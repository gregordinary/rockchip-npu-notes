# Per-SoC parameter sheets

The rest of `rockchip-npu-notes` describes the RK3588 NPU. Most of it is
**IP-inherent** — true of the rknpu/NVDLA-derived block on any Rockchip SoC that
carries it: the matmul-as-1×1-convolution model, the CNA→CORE→DPU sequence, the
precision-field encodings, the BS/BN/EW/LUT datapath, the tile-layout algebra, the
"no on-chip layout conversion" and "no hardware gather" facts. Those do not change
from chip to chip and are stated once, in the subsystem docs.

What **does** change per SoC lives here, one sheet per chip:

- **Machine parameters** — CBUF banks and bank size, the matmul tile cap, the
  weight tile-group sizes, the usable-datatype menu, the core count. These are the
  values `rocket-userspace`'s `rocket_hw_profile` collects.
- **The register offset map** — the numeric CNA/CORE/DPU register offsets. These are
  IP-**revision**-specific, not shared across every Rockchip NPU: the RK3576 NPU
  re-packs the CNA block relative to the RK3588 (see [rk3576.md](rk3576.md)). The
  *block bases* (CNA at core+0x1000, CORE at +0x3000) and the register *semantics*
  are shared; the within-block offsets are not.
- **SoC integration** — physical base addresses, clocks, resets, power domains,
  IRQs, IOMMU wiring. Pure device-map facts.

| RK3576 | 2 | Runs on hardware. Its int8 **direct** conv encoder computes bit-exactly across a swept geometry envelope, with every boundary closed by construction. Its fp16 convolution computes bit-exactly too and delivers every output channel — `rocket_rk3576_plan_ic()` splits an arbitrary `ic` into the 16-channel slices one task contracts, bit-exact against a CPU model at `ic` 16/32/64/128, `k` 1/3/5, planes to 56x56 — at `ic/16` submits. One bound sets the envelope: the DPU's output element stride is `16/ic` words, so 16 is the contraction width at which an element lands in its own two bytes. Its **first conv** — a packed image of four or fewer channels, which is a different CNA program rather than the same one at a small channel count — computes bit-exactly in fp16 over image channels 1-4, kernels 1-7, both strides and planes to 224x224 — the stem a vision model opens with, which has no single-task plan and runs through the row window, on a float weight cube of four lanes per (output channel, tap); one such program delivers 32 output channels and no more, so the library splits the output channels around it. Its **int8** form computes too, so a QUANTIZED stem runs and is the one to use — 3.6 ms against the fp16 form's 13.25 ms at the same 224x224 stride-2 shape, because every wide-output program on this part poisons its next submit and pays a power-cycle guard on every call after the first, while an int8-output one has no such hazard. The int8 cube was read off the part rather than out of a capture — one byte per weight, output channels grouped by 32, the tap row outside that group and the tap column folded in beside the four lanes — and one int8 program delivers 64 output channels where one float program delivers 32. Three geometry bounds come with it and two are silent, so all three are refused: a non-zero left pad (`CNA_PAD_CON0` bits[15:8] alone decide whether the DPU writes at all), an output width of exactly `iw/stride`, and an output width that is also a multiple of 16 — jointly the ONNX symmetric-SAME convention. An asymmetric output EXTENT is NOT one of them and is programmed: the emitter derives the trailing pad into `CNA_PAD_CON0`'s high bytes from one shared helper for both sub-encodings, and the packed converter honours it — 8 asymmetric cells bit-exact over kernels 5/7, strides 1/2, planes 64/128/224 and each axis alone, scored by pad region. A fourth bound is the INPUT ZERO POINT and it is an AXIS: this sub-encoding's pad COLUMNS feed the MAC a constant 0 at both ends and at every row whatever `CNA_PAD_CON1` holds, while its pad rows and corners honour the register at both ends and the direct path honours it on both axes, so at a non-zero zero point the interior stays bit-exact and every output whose window reaches a pad column does not — and no register reaches those columns, over a one-at-a-time walk of the seventeen the two sub-encodings differ on. The columns are MATERIALISABLE and the ENTRY does it: it extends the image at the zero point, shifts the window so the one column the hardware still synthesizes is read only by a discarded output, and crops the surplus in the de-scatter it already runs, bit-exact against the direct lowering over kernels 3/5/7, both strides, model pads 1/2/3 and both signs of zero point, at the caller's own geometry.  Its surface is a plane inside WIDER ROWS, starting at column 1 of them, and the PPU can be told so — it carries what the windows consume (`0x600C`) and the DDR line stride (`0x7024`) in different registers and honours a pitch above the extent, over 16 cells at gaps of +1/+4/+16/+64 and 12 cells of column offset, each against a control that leaves the pitch derived and differs. So a POOL consumer reads that surface in place and ResNet-18 is **6.6 ms in one submit, 1.8-1.9 ms and 20% under the direct stem's**. A CONVOLUTION consumer has no such register (the CNA's feature DMA carries the row's granule count and its line stride as one quantity in two units) and refuses the cube, so the encoding choice stays the frontend's. Where it does apply it is much the cheaper program, and for a decoded reason: a convolution's execution on this part is its PROGRAMMED MAC count — `execution = 0.218 ms + 1.246e-3 ms per programmed MMAC` (0.803 TMAC/s), fitted across a 49x kernel sweep at constant feature bytes and reproducing an independent output-channel sweep to 0.05 ms, with the feature DMA worth only 8.4% at a stem — and the packed image contracts four lanes where the direct cube contracts a 32-channel group, so at a 224x224 k7 s2 stem it programs 8.0x fewer MACs and costs `exec + drain` of 0.251 ms against 1.803 at the same 1.4-1.6e-3 ms/MMAC rate. That lever only landed once the encoding was given a RESIDENT handle and a NEON plane interleave, neither of them encoding work: as a whole prepacked call, resident on both arms with the host governor pinned, the packed form is 1.24 ms against the direct lowering's 3.09 at Inception V1's own stem and 0.63 against 1.13 at a k3 one — where transient and scalar it measured as a LOSS at both. On the GRAPH it is worth 2.1 ms: Inception V1 goes 10.7-10.8 to **8.7 ms** byte-identical in the same one submit and one 81-layer kick (7.1 today, the guard having since become cheaper) (and 7.7 to 5.6 with the guard narrowed, 6.2 to 4.1 per-kick), the saving splitting into 1.48 ms of the part running fewer MACs and 0.44 ms of host operand packing — the direct lowering scatters the image into a 32-channel cube where the packed one interleaves three bytes per pixel. It is bit-exact at a non-zero weight zero point too. Reachability is ONE graph of four and that is measured rather than derived: the gate attempts the pack for every `ic <= 4` convolution and prints what came back, and the other three stems are refused for the part's own reasons (a zero leading pad on both MobileNets, an input zero point of -128 on ResNet-18, which the column materialisation above unblocks and which pays 1.8-1.9 ms there once a pool consumer is told the row pitch). It also HEADS a chained stream (20 of 20), so it can sit inside a one-kick graph, though it can only begin a run: its feature buffer is an interleaved image and no producer's surface is one. One image channel is not among them: the emitted program will not fetch a one-byte-per-pixel row, since the feature DMA's width field carries `line_stride - 1` and that is right only from ic=2 up, so the entry programs a single channel as TWO — a zero lane against zero weights — bit-exact to 224x224 with ic=2's envelope. What broke it open was submitting a captured op stream VERBATIM: a register-identical emitter wrote nothing at every geometry tried and a leave-one-out revived none of it, while the vendor's own program wrote its whole surface — so the mode worked and the extrapolation away from the captured geometry did not. The CNA converter's offset registers are inert, so the packed image is plain two's complement and the input zero point folds into the coefficient `A` term. The fp16 register program is NOT the int8 one at another precision: there is no kernel-width channel fold, the CBUF entry count loses that fold's lookahead granule, the row stride takes the element size and the CVT is bypassed — and one vendor float value, the BS operand reader `0x501C`, makes the DPU write nothing at all when emitted against this library's coefficient group, so a capture is an oracle for a whole program rather than for a register spliced into a different one. Its **depthwise** conv computes bit-exactly too, over channel counts 8-256 (including non-multiples of 32), kernels 1-7, both strides and odd planes — its registers came from manufactured captures, but its coefficient group and int8 weight cube are neither the direct path's nor in any capture and had to be read off the part. **Matmul** computes at int8 through `rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576()`, whose output is int8 through the DPU's requant. K past one task's contraction is SPLIT, through the 32-bit writer: its budget is one 16-byte atom per (16-channel block, pixel) whatever the OUTPUT element width is, so most channels never reach DDR, and a wider programmed `oc` plus a weight-cube scatter turn the surface back into a plain cube — bit-exact to K=16384. What the budget IS a function of is the DPU's own operand width, and widening it halves the scatter to 2x, which doubles the N tile and halves the submits. A row task's output surface is bounded at 8 KiB, and that bound is over the whole PLANE — `iw * oh_full * oc_prog < 4096` — which a row split can honour only by shortening `oh_full`: on a shape whose plane comes out `Mx1` a cap stated in rows alone is met by every task while the surface is M times over, and the wide writer then returns a silently part-right matrix on most natural shapes. Stated correctly the bound costs that writer exactly the submits its wider N tile was reached for, so the planner takes the narrow one on all 315 shapes of an M/K/N sweep, and a plane too wide for even a one-row task is refused rather than run. BOTH writers intermittently emit atoms as zeros, and the rate is a property of the MEMORY SYSTEM rather than of the program: it goes from 11% of row tasks to 45% under host streaming-memcpy load, while the count of atoms never emitted at all — the poisoning — does not move, which is what separates the two hazards. A dropped atom and a legitimately zero accumulator are the same bytes and repetition does not tell them apart, so the library settles them against the arithmetic: a sentinel makes "never emitted" a per-atom fact, the task is redone once, and from the second attempt the zero atoms' accumulators are computed on the CPU over that K slice and the surface kept only if they really are zero. **The poisoning is ONE hazard and every wide output carries it**: measured with a canary that separates a dead program from a poisoned one, the fp16 first conv, the fp16 direct conv and both int32 writers each leave the next submit of any kind writing nothing, while all three int8-output paths leave it alone — one output byte never carries it and wider always does. A sacrificial submit does not absorb it, and the register condition is decoded by a JOINT sweep rather than a leave-one-out: the int32 writers carry it in one register (DPU `0x4010`'s `out`) and the fp16 path in two bits TOGETHER (DPU `0x4038[4]` and `0x4050[17]`, neither doing anything alone, which is why every one-at-a-time sweep came back empty) — but those two bits ARE the float arithmetic, so no non-poisoning fp16 encoding exists and the guard can only be paid for. What is decoded is the price: the guard tracks `power/autosuspend_delay_ms` at twice the delay, and lowering that system-wide is NOT free — a resume costs ~230 us and every job whose inter-job gap falls between the new delay and the old pays it, a 60 Hz frame interval included. So the library DRIVES the power cycle instead of waiting for it: write a zero delay, poll `runtime_status` until it reads `suspended`, restore the caller's value. At the stock 50 ms delay that takes the fp16 stem from 226 ms to 13.25 and the int32 matmul gate from 16.2 s to 9.9, with the steady-state policy every other consumer sees unchanged. The cycle clears the hazard about 87% of the time, so what makes the guard reliable is the NUMBER of redos rather than a better cycle: over twelve fp16 gate runs the redo fired on 358 row tasks and the attempt that failed next was attempt 2 for 45 of them, 3 for 10, 4 for 2, 5 for 1 and 6 for none, every cycle confirmed to have reached `suspended` first — so a cap of four leaves about 0.6% of retried tasks failing outright and eight leaves none. A task that reads unwritten really is unwritten: a 2 ms settle before the redo rescued 0 of 67 of them while the power cycle recovered all 67. The coefficient group's asymmetric `B` term is settled: the DPU ADDS `B*sum(x)`, so a weight zero point is programmed negated. **The op library reaches this part**: `rocket_conv2d_int8_rk3576()`, `rocket_conv2d_dw_int8_rk3576()` and `rocket_conv2d_fp16_rk3576()` take row-major tensors and own the cubes, the row plan, the output-channel split, the de-scatter and the submit-loop retries, and the two public entries whose semantics the part computes — `rocket_conv2d_dw_int8()` and `rocket_conv2d_fp16()` — dispatch there. Their envelope is WIDER than the single-program emitter's by a decoded amount: the weight-slice cap is keyed on the output-channel groups one program drives, so splitting those raises the slice the part accepts and fourteen refused shapes compute, `ic*kh*kw <= 4608` becoming about 5600. Zero points are folded exactly and gated. The per-submit floor is the driver's completion poll rather than the silicon, and is settled at ~340 us — against 1654 at the stock 1 ms, 3.77x. What bounds it is not the poll rate: `PC_DONE` fires before the DPU's writes have drained, so the per-job IOMMU detach faulted writes still in flight (fixed by keeping the domain attached) and the fence was signalled before the writes were visible. Both halves are closed. The visibility one is closed by retiring on the DPU's own completion — `RAW_STATUS` `DPU_0`/`DPU_1`, what the RK3588 takes as its interrupt, which sets 82-343 us after `PC_DONE` here, scaling with the bytes in flight — which lets the period drop to 50 us. A task whose DPU output element is wider than one byte raises no DPU completion at all, so the wait is bounded and those retire on `PC_DONE` after a grace period. That grace is a deadline rather than a settle, so it also covers the drain of every job that DOES raise a completion — a narrow-output matmul fails 10 runs of 10 at 200 us and none at 250, and the drain reaches 745 us on the int8 stem at 224x224 k7 s2 — which is why the default is 500 and not the 250 the fp16 path would prefer. **The host cube scatter was most of the wall, not the submit**: packing row-major tensors into this part's cubes an element at a time through the index functions cost a divide, a modulo and a call per byte, which at K=N=1024 was twelve of that matmul's thirteen milliseconds. At `kh=kw=1` three of those scatters are contiguous runs at both ends, so the matmul's feature pack, weight pack and output de-scatter became blocked memcpys, and the convolution entries — a real CHW-against-NC1HWC2 transpose — hoisted the per-element division out to one pair per channel. Bit-exact throughout, and against a single-threaded scalar CPU reference the matmul went 1.0x to 2.6x at M=32 and 6.0x to 12.9x at M=256, the 1x1 convolutions 2.2x to 5.3-21.7x, the 3x3 direct convolutions about double, and depthwise 1.4-1.6x to 2.1-2.9x. **The CHW <-> NC1HWC2 transpose is a 16x16 NEON block, and eight live vectors is the point**: written as one array of sixteen `uint8x16_t` it needs thirty-two registers with its scratch, so the compiler spills the array to the stack and it measures 3x slower than the same transpose done as two 8x8 halves plus a swap of the off-diagonal quadrants. The full-group predicate is a constant argument to one `always_inline` helper, so one source yields both the unpredicated sixteen-channel loop and the partial-group one; a runtime per-lane predicate costs 10-25%. On the graph it is 1.41x and bit-identical — 30.1 ms to ~21, feature scatter 10.91 to 4.94 and de-scatter 7.90 to 5.30 — which makes the SUBMIT the largest single bucket at 41% for the first time on this path. What is left in those two buckets is the bytes and not the loop: the de-scatter's PREP_BO drain is only 0.53 ms of its 5.3, and at ~5 MB each way both run at 2.1-2.4 GB/s against this part's single-thread memcpy ceiling of 4.4. **A tensor then stays in CUBE LAYOUT between consecutive layers and both buckets go**: a direct conv's output surface stride is `ow*oh` exactly and a feature cube's channel-group stride is `ih*iw`, so layer n's surface IS layer n+1's cube byte for byte whenever the plane and the channel rounding agree. Three refusals bound it — more than one output-channel tile (each owns its own surface BO), a padded surface stride (the depthwise path writes `round4(ow*oh)`, so 7x7 breaks and 112/56/28/14 do not), and an input channel count that is not a multiple of 32 (a handle's own cube relies on the zero it memset into the padding channels) — and the linking has to be a pre-pass over already-packed handles, since a producer that skipped its de-scatter for a consumer that then refused the cube would have left no row-major tensor at all. 24 joins of 30 layers, 20.9-21.4 ms to 11.9-12.3, 1.73x, on the same 40 submits, with the de-scatter bucket down to 0.8 ms and the SUBMIT at 62% of the library time. **A run of cube-linked layers then goes out as ONE hardware kick** — a chained stream honours read-after-write between its programs, measured over five planes from 2 KB to 387 KB with the second program's feature cube being the first's output surface, so the cube link (which means there is no host work between two layers at all) is the eligibility test. 10.4-10.5 ms to 6.7, 29 submits to 6 on the same 40 tasks, byte-identical. Two traps write a full and plausible wrong surface: both trailer fields describe the NEXT segment, which a uniform-length chain cannot tell and mixed layer programs can; and re-stamping the last layer's surface is only safe when the chain rewrites it before anyone reads it, which is false for a last layer that leaves a cube. How long a chained stream may be is the KERNEL'S COMPLETION DEADLINE rather than a property of the part: one `PC_DONE` covers a batched job's whole stream while `dpu_grace_us` is measured on a job of one task, so a stream whose work outlasts it has its fence signalled while the core is still writing — the caller reads a surface that never landed and the next job is programmed into a core that has not finished the last one. The deadline three cross-layer streams of one graph need is under 200 us, 200-500 us and 650-700 us at 34, 30 and 35 programs, not monotone in the count and not predictable from the stream, with the 500 us default sitting inside the range. `patches/rk3576/npu/0022` scales it with the tasks one `PC_DONE` covers, after which a uniform 3x3 stream at 128 channels chains 40 deep where it first failed at 4 and a mixed one 220 programs deep, with the taint word unmoved; a job of one task keeps exactly the deadline it had, so the per-op gates, the submit floor and the cost table do not move. The DMA write error whose `WARN_ON` in the job IRQ handler tainted the kernel and left the IOMMU wedged for every later job was downstream of that early fence and is not an independent defect. The cost is the fence no longer being early — the graph goes 5.0 to 6.3 ms in one fewer submit, which is the outstanding NPU work the old fence let the host run ahead of. The convolution entries' WEIGHT side was still per-element after that, and running a graph rather than a shape is what showed it: the per-output-channel filter sums and the weight cube scatter were 90% of the layers that cost most while the submit was 1-3%, and straightening both took the graph from 146 ms to 115. A graph's wall is host arithmetic over the WEIGHTS, not dispatch, AT 40 SUBMITS -- the three most expensive layers are 5, 1 and 1 of them, so `oc` tiling is not what they spend -- but that ratio is a measurement of that graph and not a property of the part, and the levers below invert it: on the graphs that run today the host operand packing is 6-12% of the wall and the two live terms are the submit-and-wait (61-81%) and the WRITE GUARD (10-23% once narrowed onto ranged cache-maintenance ioctls). **And the submit bucket is the part RUNNING PROGRAMS**: split at the driver's own events, everything that is not execution -- the ioctl, the scheduler wakeup, the register programming, the write drain and the fence signal -- is 0.08-0.17 ms, 1-4% of the bucket, and the ~340 us per-submit floor does not appear inside a one-kick graph at all. **Packing a layer's weights ONCE through a handle then takes 65% of what remained** -- 98.8 ms to 33.6, 2.94x on the same 40 submits, bit-identical (`rocket_conv2d_int8_pack_rk3576()` / `_prepacked_rk3576()`, one code path with the per-call entries rather than a second implementation, since a transient call builds the same handle and packs each output-channel tile as its loop reaches it). Only 29 ms of that 65 is the weight arithmetic the profile predicted, and **closing the profile's accounting is what found the rest**: the profiler ended at the de-scatter while the BO teardown happens after it, so 16.1 ms per inference -- unmapping a megabyte-sized dirty mapping per tile, per layer, per inference -- had been attributed to nothing at all. The feature bucket then halved with its scatter loop untouched, because 16 of its 28 ms were the allocation, the zeroing memset and the two cache ioctls, which settles the open question the other way: the feature pack was not mostly the scatter, it was the buffer. A handle freezes its fd (a BO belongs to its file and an IOVA is per-fd, so a foreign fd would submit successfully against meaningless addresses and is refused), its output-channel tile (the cube's group count follows the tile) and its quant contract. Gate residency against the ARITHMETIC rather than the wall: 173 shapes through pack plus TWO prepacked calls compared element for element, two because the second call is the first to reuse a held cube and a held surface. The rest of the op library has no encoder for this part and REFUSES rather than emitting the RK3588 program, which this part completes without writing anything. Batching a tile's row tasks into ONE submit — the obvious way to amortize a ~340 us floor paid once per row window — needed a kernel patch rather than a userspace change: a drm task descriptor carries ONE PC program whatever the regcmd stream holds, because the driver programmed `PC_TASK_CON` with `TASK_NUMBER = 1` per descriptor, so task 0 landed and task 1 was the first missing, deterministically. With the joint layout contract in place (userspace lays the programs out contiguously and links each trailer to the next, the driver programs `TASK_NUMBER = n`) it is DEFAULT-ON and worth `(n-1) * ~340 us` per call: about 3% over a per-op gate, where most shapes plan into one or two windows, and 9-11% on a graph. The part has TWO cores, and the second one is a hazard rather than a lever: it binds, reads core 0's version word and runs jobs, but two worker fds are worth 1.3-1.7x on ONE core as well — the gain is submit pipelining, since a single core is not saturated by one fd at a ~340 us floor — and two rocket jobs IN FLIGHT AT ONCE compute wrong answers on 96-100% of calls, grossly, over as much as 94% of the surface. The condition is simultaneous EXECUTION, and the correspondence is one to one against a driver-side count of jobs that started while another core had one in flight — read that counter rather than `runtime_active_time`, which reads full on both cores in every cell because both stay powered. The window is the job's own ~340 us submit (delaying one worker: zero wrong from +750 us), and it crosses processes, so no userspace mitigation exists. Four mechanisms are refuted: residual per-core state (the serial cell is clean, including with differing weights per worker, which is the cell that can see an operand swap), the two register banks being one aliased twice, the IOMMU domain (separate MMUs, and forcing every context onto one shared domain still corrupts with zero domain swaps), and the vendor's RK3576-only `state_init` plus its `pc_dma_ctrl` serialization applied as a whole delta. What is left is a shared datapath resource. Holding one worker fixed and sweeping the other's footprint names it as the CBUF feature staging area — the trigger is not capacity (a four-granule aggressor still corrupts three quarters of the victim's calls) and the extent is the feature plane specifically, not the weight cube and not how long the intruder held anything. But PLACEMENT does not separate the two cores, so the collision is not two jobs writing the same granules. The CBUF base is a real, honoured address — the low half of `0x103c` and `0x1040` is a granule offset, and a task computes at every base up to `4096 + F - plane` and fails 64 granules past it, at four planes measured — and moving one job's staging fully clear of the other's plane leaves the corruption flat at 68-78%, with the overlap counter non-zero throughout and a base past a job's own bound taking it to 100% self-wrong as the positive control. Nothing else in `CNA_CBUF_CON0` is a bank base (bits 30-31 inert, bit 29 live but corrupting), and the NPU GRF carries no per-core CBUF field at all, only a shared 16-bank clock gate. So no userspace-reachable partition exists and an address partition would not have helped; what fits every measurement is contention on a shared feature-write path rather than shared storage, NVDLA giving the CBUF separate feature and weight write ports [hypothesis]. It is not a silicon impossibility — the vendor schedules independent jobs on both cores by design — but nothing an encoder emits reaches it. They also share ONE power domain, so the wide-output poison guard must suspend EVERY bound core: the domain collapses only once both are runtime-suspended, and a guard that waited on the first device let poisoned submits through. **TWO WHOLE NETWORKS run**: MobileNetV1-224 at int8, 27 convolutions plus a PPU pooling layer and a classifier, every one of the 29 compute layers bit-exact against a CPU model of the part's own arithmetic, **4.9-5.1 ms per inference in ONE submit, with resident weights, the tensors kept in cube layout between layers, the WHOLE graph going out as one hardware kick and the pooling layer retiring on the PPU's own completion** (5.0 ms of that on a driver whose batched fence retires early, 6.2 ms before the pooling flag, 10.4-10.5 ms at one submit per layer, 21 ms with the transposes at every boundary, 115 ms with no resident weights at all), returning TFLite's own top-1 and whole top-5. **A POOL RAISES NO DPU COMPLETION** — `PC_OPERATION_ENABLE` is a per-block bitmap and a pooling program enables PPU and PPU_RDMA with `0x60` against a convolution's `0x1d`, so it has no DPU stage and every pooling job retired on the `dpu_grace_us` deadline in full (641/389/213 us as the parameter moved 500/250/100); `DRM_ROCKET_JOB_PPU_DONE` names the class and the wait is 71 us. The PPU also honours a source surface stride that is NOT a multiple of four, which is what makes a cube-in pool possible at all — a direct conv's surface stride is `ow*oh` where every vendor pooling program carries `round4`, 49 against 52 at a 7x7 plane. Afterwards the 23-layer kick is 81% of the wall and nothing else reaches 6%. Against TFLite the graph differs on 0.095% of elements and every one of them by a single count — that residue is the requant rather than a defect, since this DPU's OUT_CVT carries a 15-bit multiplier and rounds ties to even where TFLite carries 31 bits and rounds half away from zero, and the ranking survives what the last bit does not. **And ResNet-18-224**, a third and different question — 3x3-heavy where both MobileNets are 1x1-heavy, with a 7x7 stem, a max pool, a fused ReLU on every add and torchvision's explicit padding (a lead pad of 3 against a trailing 2, which TFLite's SAME never produces): 32 layers, every compute layer bit-exact, TFLite's own top-1, 0.0386% of elements at one count, 7.4 ms in ONE submit (9.2 with the direct stem lowering). **None of the three cube-join refusal classes its residual topology produced was a property of the part**: a skip source may write a cube whenever every layer that reads it can read one (a resident handle owns its surface, so the lifetime a distant reader needs is already there), an add's two operands may be placed in either order (the buffer is one allocation per add and each slice has one writer, so the order the producers run in never mattered), and a POOL is a cube producer like any convolution. All twelve close and every adjacent pair of all three networks joins. No per-tensor-quantized ResNet-18 is published, so `mkresnet18.py` builds one from a timm checkpoint and ASSERTS the two properties the gate depends on. It found two register facts and, twice over, one harness defect a MobileNet cannot reach. **Neither operand of a graph layer is "the layer before"** — three of its convolutions take operand A from a producer three layers back, against 0 in either MobileNet, so on those two resolving by position and resolving by producer are the same function and no gate can fail; the second occurrence surfaced as an unrelated per-axis top-5 that read like an accuracy result, and what separated the two was running the graph on the CPU three ways (`rk3576_net_gate hostchain`), which prices the requant at 2 int8 logit counts against the wiring's 120. A per-axis ResNet-18 returns TFLite's own top-1. **And Inception V1-224 is a fourth**, chosen for the axes it VARIES rather than for size: a concat/split topology (nine four-operand joins), nine producers with four readers, a k=7 pooling kernel, thirteen pooling layers, and 18+18 layers whose operand A is not the layer before. All 81 of its compute layers are bit-exact on the first run and it returns TFLite's own top-1, 0.0123% of elements apart and all by one count — a concatenation is PLACEMENT rather than arithmetic when its operands already carry the output's quantization. What its topology costs is the chain, and none of it was the part's: with the concatenations built on the host 34 of 80 adjacent pairs join, in 69 submits at **19.8 ms**. Wired as PLACED SLICES — one buffer per concatenation, a slice per operand, every consumer reading the buffer as its cube, and a third chain node kind (`placement`) so a layer with no program does not break the run it sits in — every refusal closes and the whole graph is **ONE hardware kick in ONE submit at 10.8 ms**. That geometry was already inside the int8 correctness envelope, in six swept shapes; what kept it out of a graph was the descriptor, which could only say "derive the output from a symmetric pad", and the row-major buffer it forced is what broke the cube chain at every stride-2 depthwise layer. `rocket_conv2d_desc.oh/.ow` now name the extent (zero meaning the symmetric derivation, so nothing existing changes) and the bound is the KERNEL: a trailing pad of `kh` or more is an output row whose whole window is pad, and it is refused, as is an extent the fp16 and RK3588 paths did not derive themselves. A chip can need two sheets. The RK3576's parameter sheet covers identity, SoC
integration and the state of the port; its register encoding is large enough — and

[porting-patterns.md](porting-patterns.md) is the cross-chip sheet: what the RK3588 and
RK3576 pair establishes about porting to a third part. Which classes of claim transfer
as reliable priors (the datapath algebra), which are coin flips and must be re-measured

re-pack is built from, and the method for matching a register by value and function
rather than by offset. Read it before assuming any RK3588 fact on a new SoC.

An IP-inherent fact that turns out to differ on a new chip is a bug in the claim, not
a per-SoC parameter — promote the correction into the subsystem doc and note the SoC
that disproved the old scope. The RK3576 CNA re-pack did exactly that to the old
"register offsets are not SoC-specific" claim.
