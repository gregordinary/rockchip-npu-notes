# RK3576 — SoC parameter sheet

The RK3576 NPU runs through the **same mainline `rocket` drm/accel driver** as the
RK3588, with an in-flight RFC series adding the SoC. This sheet is identity, SoC
integration and the state of the port: what external reverse-engineering and the RFC
establish, and what an H96 MAX M9 confirms live. The register layer — the encoding
written out as an emitter, and what hardware settles about it — is
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md). The load-bearing difference from the RK3588 is the
**re-packed CNA register offset map**.

Provenance: Ga Hing Woo's mainline bring-up (blog + `github.com/gahingwoo/linux-rk3576-npu`,
including `notes/rk3576-npu-values.md`); the RFC series "accel/rocket: RK3576 NPU (RKNN)
enablement" (Jiaxing Hu, patchwork `linux-rockchip` series 1129921, 8 patches,
2026-07-18); and the Radxa RK3576 vendor kernel `linux-6.1-stan-rkr5.1`. The load-bearing
documents in that repo are `vendor-capture/RK3576_CNA_MAP.md` and the CORE/DPU map,
`predict.py`, `FINDINGS-FLOATSURFACE.md`, `MATMUL-PIPELINE-ANALYSIS.md`, and
`vendor_regcmd_full.txt` — a full 139-entry vendor register program.

## NPU identity

- rknpu-derived, **2 cores** (`npu0`, `npu1`), marketed 6 TOPS. The RK3588 has 3 cores.
- Same `rocket` uAPI and `/dev/accel/accel0`; no interface change in userspace. The
  mainline `rocket` core landed in kernel 6.18; RK3576 support is the RFC on top.
  [source-confirmed: gahingwoo repo — kernel fragment `CONFIG_DRM_ACCEL + ROCKET + CRC32C`]
- DT compatible string: `rockchip,rk3576-rknn-core`. [source-confirmed: RFC patch 1]
- **Core 1 binds and runs jobs, and buys nothing — measure the second core against a
  one-core control, not against one fd.** With core 1 enabled at its real address
  (`0x27708000`, see "Live-hardware notes") and given the six clocks
  `rocket_core_init()` hard-gets, the kernel fans an open fd's work across both cores'
  schedulers with no userspace change, jobs land on both, and each core reports an equal
  share of runtime-active time. Two worker fds are worth 1.3-1.7x over one — but **that
  is submit pipelining, and one core delivers it on its own**: with core 1 unbound, two
  fds give 1.51-1.69x at 8x64x32, 1.62-1.66x at 32x1024x512, 1.49-1.51x at 128x1024x1024
  and 1.33x at 256x512x2048, matching or beating the same measurement with both cores
  bound (1.55-1.74x, 1.49-1.68x, 1.38-1.42x). A single core is not saturated by one fd at
  a 439 us submit floor, so filling its dispatch gaps is the whole gain and a second core
  has nothing left to add. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, 2026-07-28, `tests/rk3576_multicore.c`]
- **Two rocket jobs IN FLIGHT AT ONCE compute wrong answers, and that is the reason to
  leave core 1 disabled.** The condition is simultaneous EXECUTION — not the second core's
  existence, not anything a job inherits from the one before it, and not the mapping.
  Driven through a mutex so that only one job is ever in flight — two fds, both cores
  bound, jobs alternating cores — every call is exact: 96 calls a cell at 256x512x2048,
  32x1024x512 and 128x1024x1024. The same two fds driving at once are wrong on **96-100%
  of calls** over as much as 94% of the surface, with deltas spanning the whole int8
  range. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_core_pair.c pair`]
- **The correspondence with overlap is one to one, and it is read from the driver rather
  than inferred.** Both cores report runtime-active time for the whole wall in every cell,
  because both stay resumed throughout — that says they are POWERED, not that a cell's
  jobs were spread over them, and a cell whose jobs all landed on one core is the one-core
  control wearing a different label. An instrumented `rocket` counts jobs per core, domain
  swaps per core, and jobs started while another core already had one in flight. At
  256x512x2048 one library call is one job, and the concurrent cell runs 48 jobs a core
  with 48 overlap events — every job in a simultaneous pair — against 96 of 96 calls
  wrong. Every clean cell records zero overlap. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]
- **The vulnerable window is the job's own duration, so the contended resource is held for
  the whole of it rather than for a phase.** Delaying one worker past a common barrier,
  one call each per rep: 100% of calls wrong at +0 and +100 us, 91.7% at +250, 4.2% at
  +500, and **zero from +750 us out to +5 ms** — a sharp cliff, and the driver's overlap
  count reaches zero at the same rung. The per-submit floor is 439 us, so the window is
  the submit. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_core_pair.c stagger`]
- **It crosses processes, so no userspace mitigation exists.** Two processes holding one
  fd each cannot be serialized by a library mutex, and they corrupt exactly as two fds in
  one process do: 96 of 96 calls wrong, against zero for a single process doing the same
  total work. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_core_pair.c xproc`]
- **Four candidate mechanisms are refuted, and the refutations are worth keeping.**
  - *Residual per-core register state.* Anything a program inherits from a previous one
    would corrupt the serial cell too, and the serial cell is clean — including the cell
    where the two workers hold DIFFERENT weights, which is the one that can see a worker
    being handed the other's operands. The plain matrix gives both workers the same
    operands, so run the differing-weights cell before concluding anything about inputs.
  - *The two register banks being one aliased twice.* They are distinct hardware: write
    different values into core 0's and core 1's PC `TASK_DMA_BASE_ADDR` and CNA
    `S_POINTER` and both read back what was written to them.
  - *The IOMMU domain.* Three ways. Each core has its own MMU (`27702000` and `2770a000`)
    in its own group, so no attach can displace the other's. Making every context share
    ONE domain — zero domain swaps in every cell, confirmed by the counter — leaves the
    concurrent cell at 96 of 96 wrong. And a page-table mix-up would show in the
    differing-weights serial cell, which is clean across 36 domain swaps a core.
  - *The vendor's RK3576-only `state_init` and its `pc_dma_ctrl` PC serialization.*
    Applied together as a whole delta — seeding both CNA pointer groups at every power-on
    and holding a device-global lock across the whole register-programming sequence — the
    concurrent cell is 100% wrong against a 97.9% control. The seeding is counted, so this
    is a negative about the vendor's sequence and not about a knob that never fired. A
    one-at-a-time pass over these could not have found a condition of two, which is why
    they were applied jointly; since the whole delta does nothing, no subset can.
- **What is left is a datapath resource the two cores share, and the TRM names one
  candidate.** There is exactly one `CBUF` on this part: 1 MB at `0x3fe80000` in the
  address map, outside either core's register bank; its 16 banks are gated from a single
  `NPU_GRF_MEMGATE_CON0` field ("clock gate enable for cbuf bank 0-15") in the shared NPU
  GRF; and the CRU carries one `ACLK_RKNN_CBUF`/`HCLK_RKNN_CBUF` pair which both cores'
  nodes list. [TRM] The vendor driver is the only Rockchip NPU configuration with a
  `cache_sgt_init`, and what `rk3576_cache_sgt_init()` does is hand each core a disjoint
  slice of exactly that megabyte — core 0 `[0,448K)` and `[896K,960K)`, core 1
  `[448K,896K)` and `[960K,1024K)`. [source-confirmed: `rknpu_drv.c:123`, table
  `block_size_kb = {448, 64, 448, 64}` at `block_offset_kb = {0, 896, 448, 960}`, indexed
  `[i * core_num + j]` with `core_num = num_irqs = 2`] **At 16 banks over 1 MB that is
  64 KiB a bank, and the split is bank-aligned**: core 0 gets banks `{0-6, 14}` and core 1
  `{7-13, 15}`, eight each. This library's own single-core CBUF pool measures 448 KiB
  (7168 granules of 64 B) — bank for bank the vendor's core-0 primary block, measured with
  core 1 unbound and with no knowledge of the vendor table. And the
  CBUF banking is programmed by the *register program*: on the RK3588 `CNA_CBUF_CON0`
  carries `DATA_BANK`, `WEIGHT_BANK` and `FC_DATA_BANK` bank selects over 16 banks, and on
  the RK3576 the same register carries a granule allowance F — an allowance, not a bank
  base. Nothing an encoder emits depends on the core, so both cores stage into the same
  banks. That is a hypothesis with a named mechanism, not a measurement: nothing here has
  watched the two cores write the same CBUF address, and the 448 KiB coincidence points
  both ways — a hardware per-core partition would mean the two cores *cannot* collide and
  would refute the candidate, where one core's addressing reaching 448 KiB of a shared
  pool would confirm it.

  **Two distinctions keep this honest.** The word `cbuf` appears nowhere in the vendor
  driver: the partition is expressed as an allocator plus an IOMMU mapping
  (`rknpu_iommu_map_with_cache_sgt`, `rknpu_remap_pfn_with_cache_sgt`), never as a register
  write. And whether a job's buffers land in that region is an alloc-flag opt-in
  (`rknpu_gem.c`, the `nbuf_size > 0` branch). So `cache_sgt` governs
  CBUF-as-allocatable-memory, which is a different use of the same RAM from
  CBUF-as-staging, and it is not what keeps vendor staging from colliding — an external
  RK3576 YOLOv8 benchmark runs two per-core contexts correctly *without* the SRAM opt-in
  (see the concurrency bullet below). No other public effort covers it either — the one other mainline
  RK3576 NPU bring-up (gahingwoo, `linux-rk3576-npu`, ROCK 4D) runs a single core, and the
  wall it stops at is the chained-task one that `patches/rk3576/npu/0008` closes.
- **It is not a silicon impossibility, and the missing piece is not expressible in the
  current uAPI.** The vendor driver schedules independent jobs on the two RK3576 cores
  concurrently by design — a todo list and an in-flight job per core, with
  `RKNPU_CORE_AUTO_MASK` load-balancing by queued task count — and the SDK exposes
  per-core context placement alongside its combined mode. [source-confirmed:
  `rknpu_job.c`, `rknn_api.h`] So the part is meant to run two jobs at once. An external
  benchmark measures it doing so: a reComputer RK3576 YOLOv8 run
  (hackster.io/wurengmin) puts two `spawn` processes on separate cores via
  `init_runtime(core_mask=NPU_CORE_0)` against `NPU_CORE_1`, releases them together
  through a barrier, and reports ~2x throughput over 394 frames x 5 repeats x 3 model
  sizes with plausible detections. Its script carries no numerical check — it records
  output shapes and timings and never diffs outputs across configurations — so read it as
  establishing that correct two-core concurrency is software-reachable here, not as a
  bit-exactness result. What `rocket`
  lacks is a per-core partition of whatever is shared, and on the CBUF hypothesis that
  partition lives in the *register program*, which userspace authors without knowing —
  and without being able to choose — which core will run it. The vendor's own uAPI is the
  precedent: `init_runtime(core_mask=...)` fixes the core at **context creation**, before
  any inference, so its closed runtime can bake per-core state into the program it builds.
  A fix therefore needs the core identity to reach the encoder, not just a driver patch.
- **THE RESOURCE IS THE CBUF FEATURE STAGING AREA, and the trigger and the extent are
  different things.** Every earlier cell gave both workers the same shape, so none of them
  could separate a capacity from a control path. Holding one worker fixed as a VICTIM
  (256x512x2048, 2048 feature granules) and sweeping the other's footprint as an AGGRESSOR
  does (`tests/rk3576_core_asym.c`, 160 victim calls a cell, two runs) [HW sweep, H96 MAX
  M9, measured 2026-07-28]:

  | aggressor | feature granules | weights | victim calls wrong | surface destroyed per wrong call |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | tiny  | 4    | 4 KiB    | 71.9-75.6% | 0.6-0.7% |
  | small | 32   | 16 KiB   | 56.2-70.0% | 2.7-3.0% |
  | mid   | 256  | 64 KiB   | 22.5-32.5% | 12.9-16.9% |
  | large | 1024 | 256 KiB  | 20.6-22.5% | 27.9-38.9% |
  | equal | 2048 | 1024 KiB | 95.6-98.8% | 48.5-55.3% |

  **The trigger is not capacity.** An aggressor whose whole feature plane is FOUR granules
  — 256 bytes against a 448 KiB pool — corrupts about three quarters of the victim's
  calls. Nothing is being exhausted, so the collision is unconditional.

  **The extent is the feature plane, and not the weight path or the occupancy.** Footprint
  and submit duration co-vary across those five rows, so two more shapes pull them apart:
  `wideN` (4 granules behind a 128 KiB N-tiled weight cube — small plane, long submit)
  destroys 5.6-6.5%, and `flatN` (a 2048-granule plane against 32 KiB of weights — large
  plane, short submit) destroys 30.4-34.9%. Five to six times the damage from four times
  less weight and a shorter occupancy, but 512x the feature plane.

  So the two cores stage their feature planes into the same CBUF, unconditionally, and
  what an intruder destroys is however much plane it staged. A cell is only a measurement
  once the driver's overlap counter is non-zero, which is not automatic: a small aggressor
  finishes a fixed call count inside the victim's HOST-SIDE packing and the first run of
  this recorded zero overlap for every asymmetric cell. The aggressor has to drive
  continuously for the victim's whole run.
- **PLACEMENT DOES NOT SEPARATE THE CORES, so the collision is not two jobs writing the
  same CBUF granules.** The CBUF base is a real, honoured address and moving it is not
  enough — which retires the search for a bank-select field, because the field that moves
  a job was already decoded and already emitted. The low half of `CNA_CBUF_ENTRIES`
  (`0x103c`) and of `CNA_CBUF_CON0` (`0x1040`) is a GRANULE OFFSET: where a task's window
  starts and where its fetch resumes. The row-reuse path drives it; every plain entry
  point leaves it at zero, so every job stages from granule 0.
  (`tests/rk3576_cbuf_base.c`, `ROCKET_RK3576_CBUF_BIAS` / `rocket_rk3576_set_cbuf_bias()`)
  [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-28]

  **The base is honoured, and it reaches only inside the task's own allowance window.**
  The last base at which a task still computes is `4096 + F - plane`, at every plane
  measured — planes of 64, 256, 1024 and 2048 granules, each exact at the prediction and
  wrong 64 granules past it. So a job cannot be staged outside the budget it programmed,
  and two jobs can only be placed apart when their planes together fit one window.

  **Placed apart, they corrupt exactly as before.** Victim 256x512x2048 at base 0 (2048
  granules) against the `tiny` aggressor, whose base is swept clear of it: 78% of victim
  calls wrong at base 0, and 68-72% at 2048, 3072 and 4032 — flat, inside the run-to-run
  scatter, with the driver's overlap counter at 57-60 a cell throughout. The knob is
  demonstrably live on the worker thread: a base past the aggressor's *own* bound (4156
  against a bound of 4092) takes it to 896 of 896 calls self-wrong, where every scored row
  leaves it at 2-4%. Without that positive control "biasing changed nothing" and "the bias
  never reached the emitter" are the same table.

  **Nothing else in `CNA_CBUF_CON0` is a bank base.** The emitted word is
  `bit28 | F<<16 | base`, and sweeping the only bits this library never varies leaves the
  register fully accounted for: bits 30 and 31 are inert, and bit 29 is live but corrupts
  the job at every combination that sets it. **Nor is there anywhere else to put a
  partition** — the NPU GRF carries exactly one CBUF field, `NPU_GRF_MEMGATE_CON0`
  bits[15:0] ("clock gate enable for cbuf bank 0-15"), shared rather than per core, and no
  base, mask or window register [TRM, `Rockchip_RK3576_TRM_Part1_V1.2`].

  So no userspace-reachable partition of the CBUF exists, and a perfect address partition
  would not have helped anyway. **The mechanism this leaves is a shared feature-staging
  DATAPATH rather than shared storage** [hypothesis]: this IP's ancestor gives the CBUF
  four ports, feature-write and weight-write among them separately
  [nvdla.org/hw/v1/hwarch.html], and contention on one shared feature-write path predicts
  all three measurements at once — extent proportional to the feature plane, indifference
  to weight bytes and to occupancy, and immunity to placement. Reading the CBUF SRAM at
  `0x3fe80000` while a job runs would say whether the two cores' windows sit at the same
  physical base, but that is now diagnosis and not a fix, and it is a `/dev/mem` poke at an
  SRAM in the NPU voltage domain, so the domain has to be held resumed or the SoC hangs.
  The one experiment still decisive on the mechanism is diffing the vendor's live submit
  program for one model at `core_mask = NPU_CORE_0` against `NPU_CORE_1`, which needs a
  vendor-BSP RK3576 with an rknpu regcmd dump; the H96 is mainline.
- **The envelope for callers**: one rocket job in flight at a time is safe on this part
  however many cores are bound, and two are not, whether they come from two fds or two
  processes. Single-fd work is therefore unaffected and the whole gate suite is green with
  core 1 bound; a multi-fd caller is not.
- **The library refuses to build the fan-out itself.** There is no encoding that avoids the
  corruption and nothing userspace can do about another process, so what is left is to stop
  a caller walking into it alone. `rocket_open()` counts the bound cores under
  `/sys/bus/platform/drivers/rocket`, and on this part with more than one bound it warns
  once and then returns `-EBUSY` for the **second** fd — two fds being what puts two jobs in
  flight. `ROCKET_RK3576_ALLOW_MULTI_FD=1` lifts it, for the probes that provoke the
  corruption deliberately and for a caller that has serialized its own submits. One bound
  core is left entirely alone: multi-fd is not just safe there, it is where the 1.5x lives.
  Verified both ways on the part — silent with one core bound, refusing with two, override
  honoured [HW, H96 MAX M9]. The durable fix is still `status = "disabled"` on the core 1
  DT node.
- **The corruption is gross, not sparse**, and an earlier reading of it as "a handful of
  positions off by exactly +/-2" came from checking against a CPU model of the DPU's
  integer requant: that model disagrees by one count on a fraction of a percent of
  elements in EVERY configuration, one core included, which is noise larger than the
  signal it was being used to find. Check a concurrency question against a SOLO RUN of the
  part, so the requant gap cancels. Part of that gap is now closed at the source — the
  convertor rounds ties to EVEN, where the models rounded half up
  ([out-cvt-converter.md](../encodings/out-cvt-converter.md)) — but the discipline stands
  whatever the model's remaining error is: a solo run cancels it, and a model does not. With unit operands and an identity requant the delta
  reads directly in MAC terms, and the wrong elements at the boundary of the damage are
  off by two or three terms while the bulk are off by far more — a staging corruption
  rather than an unwritten surface. Giving each worker its own weights, 3.3% of wrong
  elements hold the *other* worker's correct value against 0.4% by chance: real cross-talk,
  but not a clean swap, so the dominant effect is neither job's arithmetic.
- **The two cores share one power domain**, and that is load-bearing for the wide-output
  hazard below: the domain collapses only once BOTH cores are runtime-suspended, so a
  guard that waits on one device can let the next submit through while the other core
  still holds the domain up. Jobs land on both cores, so either can be the active one at
  any instant. Drive and wait on every bound device, not the first.
  [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

## What is shared with the RK3588

Ga Hing Woo reports the command stream matches the vendor line-for-line once the offset
map is right: the register command stream content, the state-init sequence, the
soft-reset and its IOMMU re-attach, and the submit handshake. [source] So the
IP-inherent datapath is unchanged: matmul-as-1×1-conv, the CNA→CORE→DPU sequence, the
precision-field encodings, the BS/BN/EW/LUT semantics, and the tile-layout algebra all
transfer. The **block bases** are also shared: within a core, CNA is at +0x1000 and
CORE at +0x3000, exactly as on the RK3588 (pc VERSION register at +0x0).

## What differs: the CNA register encoding

The RK3576 CNA block is not a shifted copy of the RK3588's — it is a **different
geometry-register encoding**. Three things change at once: some registers move, several
registers that are unused gaps on the RK3588 are live on the RK3576, and at shared
offsets the bit-packing differs. Barely any CNA geometry register coincides with the
RK3588 by both offset and meaning: `0x1014` (stride) and `0x1110` (weight / DCOMP address)
do. The **feature-data address does not** — the RK3576 carries it at `0x1088` on *both*
datapaths and leaves `0x1070` (the RK3588's slot) at zero. This is why a straight RK3588
regcmd generator miscomputes on the RK3576.

Provenance: the full closed-form RK3576 CNA/CORE/DPU encoder is reverse-engineered in
Ga Hing Woo's `vendor-capture/RK3576_CNA_MAP.md` (github.com/gahingwoo/linux-rk3576-npu)
by converting the same Conv2d to `.rknn` one parameter at a time and diffing the vendor
RKNN regcmd, then validated by a `predict.py` that reproduces **34/34** geometry /
channel / tiling / const registers with zero mismatch across 7 MobileNetV1 captures
(first-conv ARGB, normal `in_ch>4`, and depthwise, including the pointwise `k=1` case —
the matmul-relevant shape). It is the vendor-toolkit conv path, cross-referenced against
what Mesa emits with the RK3588 map; it has not been exercised on our matmul path or run
by us on hardware.

CNA delta, normal `in_ch>4` path (`dw` = depthwise, `wbpk` = weight bytes per kernel =
`ic·k·k·2`, or `k·k·4` for dw; `surf` = `max(in_w/4, ic/2)`). RK3588 names from
[../../rocket-userspace/include/npu_hw.h](../../rocket-userspace/include/npu_hw.h):

| Offset | RK3576 field | RK3588 register at that offset |
|---|---|---|
| `0x1014` | `(s<<3)\|s` stride | `CNA_CONV_CON3` — **coincides** (stride) |
| `0x1018` | `0x40000404` (precision/ARGB) | gap |
| `0x101C` | total weight bytes | gap |
| `0x1020` | `(dw?oc:ic)·k·k` | `CNA_DATA_SIZE0` (not DATAIN dims) |
| `0x1024` | `k_word<<16 \| (oc-1)` | `CNA_DATA_SIZE1` |
| `0x1028` | `(in_w·in_h·ic/64)<<16 \| (ic-1)` | `CNA_DATA_SIZE2` |
| `0x102C` | `(in_w-1)<<16 \| (in_h-1)` | `CNA_DATA_SIZE3` |
| `0x1030` | `wbpk<<16 \| (out_w-1)` | `CNA_WEIGHT_SIZE0` |
| `0x1034` | `out_w·out_h - 1` (universal) | `CNA_WEIGHT_SIZE1` |
| `0x1038` | `0x07` const | `CNA_WEIGHT_SIZE2` |
| `0x103C` | `surf<<16` (CBUF surface stride) | gap |
| `0x1044` | `in_w<<16 \| surf` | `CNA_CBUF_CON1` |
| `0x1048` | `0x00071C70` (Mesa hardcodes `0x000E38E0` = 2× → wrong) | gap |
| `0x1078` | `(in_w-1)<<16 \| (in_h-1)` | `CNA_DMA_CON0` |
| `0x107C` | `ic-1` | `CNA_DMA_CON1` |
| `0x108C` | `0x000F000F` const | gap |
| `0x1090` | `in_w·4` (CBUF input line stride) | gap |
| `0x1094` / `0x1098` | `in_w·in_h` (equal) | gap |
| `0x118C` | `(in_w-1)<<16 \| (in_h-1)` | gap |

The first-conv `in_ch≤4` ARGB path packs about sixteen of these differently again —
its input is a packed image rather than a cube, so `0x1090` is the packed row's
granule count `in_w·in_ch/16` and `0x1078` carries that count in its high half
instead of the width. It is its own sub-encoding, written out in full in
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).

**CORE / DPU also re-pack.** On the normal path the RK3576 CORE writes the output
geometry at `0x301C` as `(oh_task-1)<<16 | (ow-1)` — an offset the RK3588 uses for
`CORE_CLIP_TRUNCATE` — and the DPU carries the same height (`0x4024 = oh_task-1`,
`0x4034 = (oh_task-1)<<16 | (ow-1)`). The DPU `OUT_CVT` triple lands at
`0x40AC`/`0x40B0`/`0x40B4` (RK3588: `0x4080`/`0x4084`/`0x4088`) and the output address at
`0x4018` (RK3588 `DPU_DST_BASE_ADD` is `0x4020`).

The height field is **not** halved — an earlier reading of it as `(oh-1)>>1` came from a
capture whose task covered half its plane. It is the rows *this task* writes, minus one;
a single-task capture of the same conv writes the full `oh-1`. See
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md), which carries the corrected encoding as an
implemented emitter.

Still open in the capture: the tiling-derived fields (they mirror Mesa's CBUF allocator
output — `input_banks`, `data_entries`, atomics — but packed at RK3576 offsets), the
`0x1080` surf-stride formula, and the multi-task slicing where the input-height field
carries a per-slice height set by the CBUF allocator.

### What the RK3576 map reveals about RK3588 gaps

The RK3576 capture cleanly names geometry quantities the RK3588 must also configure but
which our RK3588 register map (`npu_hw.h`) leaves as gaps or drives as Mesa hardcodes:
input line stride (`0x1090` = `in_w·4`), input CBUF surface stride (`0x103c`), total
weight bytes (`0x101c`), a CBUF data-entries word (`0x1048`), the precision/ARGB format
word (`0x1018`), and duplicate feature dims (`0x118c`). Two are direct RK3588 leads.
Because the blocks re-pack, each RK3588 equivalent is a semantic-function match at a
different offset. Dumping our RK3588 matmul regcmd on-device settles it [HW, RK1]: the
equivalents exist and our generator already emits them. The line stride (`in_w·4`, RK3576
`0x1090`) is written at `CNA_DMA_CON1` `0x107c`, and the surface stride (RK3576 `0x103c`)
at `CNA_DMA_CON2` `0x1080`. RK3588 `0x1090` is itself a genuine clock-gating register
(`*_DISABLE_CLKGATE` fields per Mesa `registers.xml`) that both Mesa and our generator
leave at its reset default — so RK3576's reuse of that offset for line stride is an offset
collision, not a missing RK3588 register. The remaining RK3576-only offsets
(`0x1018`/`0x101c`/`0x1048`/`0x108c`/`0x1094`/`0x1098`/`0x118c`) are absent from Mesa's
RK3588 driver and never emitted by our bit-exact matmul — RK3576 IP additions with no
RK3588 counterpart. Net: the RK3576 map exposed **no missing RK3588 register**; the
apparent gaps are the re-pack reusing offsets that the RK3588 stream fills elsewhere or
correctly leaves at default (verified on the matmul path; the vendor conv path is
separately Mesa-validated). The `0x1048` slot where Mesa's RK3588 value is 2× the RK3576
one is a forward RK3576-porting concern (Mesa's CBUF-entries math misapplied), not an
RK3588 correctness gap. The RK3576 conv capture does not touch the other RK3588 unknowns
(`CORE_3030` `0x3030`, `DPU_40C4` `0x40C4`, `DPU_SURFACE_ADD` `0x40C0`).

## Design implication for rocket-userspace

Supporting the RK3576 needs **two** per-chip layers, not one:
1. A `rocket_hw_profile` for the RK3576 machine parameters (CBUF geometry, tile caps,
   core count, dtype menu) — the mechanism that already exists for the RK3588.
2. A per-chip **regcmd encoder for the CNA/CORE/DPU geometry registers**, not merely an
   offset remap. The differences are offset moves, different bit-packing at shared
   offsets, RK3576-only registers, and registers whose field carries a per-task quantity
   packed differently — so a single address-translation table is insufficient; the
   geometry-register emission itself is per-chip. What stays shared is the datapath
   semantics (the precision-field encodings, the BS/BN/EW/LUT meanings, the CNA→CORE→DPU
   block sequence) and the block bases — the IP-inherent parts the vendor stream matches
   line-for-line. That encoder is
   [../../rocket-userspace/src/npu_regcmd_rk3576.c](../../rocket-userspace/src/npu_regcmd_rk3576.c)
   for the int8 conv path, diffed register-for-register against the vendor programs by
   `tests/regcmd_rk3576_gate.c`. `RK3576_CNA_MAP.md`'s closed-form `predict_cna(params)`
   was its starting point; read it with the four corrections in
   [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md), each of which silently mis-programs the part.

`rocket_hw_rk3576` carries the machine parameters, and its CBUF budget is measured on
the part: 4096 granules = 256 KiB of feature capacity, which is the **default
allocation** out of a shared pool of roughly 448 KiB. A granule-allowance field in
`CNA_CBUF_CON0` raises the feature side to 384 KiB against the weight path, and the
emitter plans it per task, so a plane between the two computes exactly instead of
corrupting silently (see [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md)). The matmul tile cap and
the weight N-group are measured here too, and neither is the RK3588's: `max_tile` is
2048 output channels rather than 256, and `ngroup` is 32 rather than 16. Only
`kgroup_4b` is still carried over unmeasured, because no 4-byte input path has been run
on this part. The chip autodetect selects this profile and **warns** which datapaths
have an encoder, rather than pretending the part is supported.

The per-chip dispatch that warning describes is now real rather than advisory, and it
covers the whole op library rather than the two matmul entries it started at. The refusal
sits at the **generator** — every public `gen_*` in `npu_regcmd.c`, which is the RK3588
encoder — because the op library composes those: conv, pooling, the LUT activations, the
norms, the FFN, softmax, reduce and the ViT encoders all reach the part through them, so
one check covers every one by construction instead of by enumeration, and every call site
already treats a generator failure as fatal. `tests/rk3576_refusal_gate.c` holds it, and
the entries that have their own encoder (`rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576()` and its int32
sibling) dispatch ahead of it.

The dtype capability mask cannot do that job: it says which *precisions* a chip can run,
and the RK3576 runs int8 and fp16 perfectly well — through its own encoder. The check
that belongs at the seam is on the **encoding**.

What refusing does not do is make the part usable. The RK3576's own conv encoders compute
bit-exactly but are still reachable only from the test harnesses and from
`rocket_matmul_rk3576.c`; `rocket_conv2d_int8()` and its siblings refuse there rather than
dispatching to them. Lifting the driving logic in `tests/rk3576_conv_gate.c` — the BO
management, the operand packing, the `ic` and row planning — into the library is what
closes that, and it is the largest single gap between the reverse engineering being done
and the chip being usable.

Two details of the reference map to carry into any encoder:

- **The feature-data address is `0x1088` on both CNA datapaths.** `0x1070` reads zero in
  every capture, including the one program that provably needs a non-zero feature address
  (a row-slice whose `0x1088` carries exactly that slice's byte offset). Only the weight
  address (`0x1110`) coincides with the RK3588.
- **The vendor stream carries no PC trailer.** The captured 139-entry program is CNA +
  CORE + DPU + RDMA register writes only; the vendor kernel fires the job through its own
  `enable_mask`/`task_number` in the task descriptor. Our RK3588 regcmds end with the
  `OP_ENABLE` trailer that writes `PC_OPERATION_ENABLE`, which is what actually triggers
  compute under `rocket`. An RK3576 emitter built by transcribing the vendor capture must
  add that trailer back, or the pipeline is configured and never fires.

## The cold-start wall

`PC_TASK_CON` packs `TASK_NUMBER` in the low bits with `TASK_PP_EN`,
`TASK_COUNT_CLEAR` and one TRM-reserved bit directly above it, and the field is **12
bits wide on the RK3588 and 16 on the RK3576** — the vendor `rknpu` config carries it
as `pc_task_number_bits`. Mainline `rocket` builds the word from the RK3588 accessors
unconditionally, so on this part `0x7001` asks the PC for a **task count of 28673**
instead of 1. The PC starts that program, runs the one task it was handed and is left
mid-stream: the first submit of an NPU power session computes, and every later submit in
the same session leaves its output buffer untouched. A gap past the 50 ms runtime-PM
autosuspend hides it, because the power cycle resets the PC.

Carrying the width per SoC in `rocket_soc_data` and building the word from it clears it,
and costs the RK3588 nothing — at 12 bits it still emits `0x7001`, bit for bit. With an
int8 conv submitted back to back with no gap, **1 of 8 submits produced output before, 8
of 8 after** (32 of 32 on a longer soak), byte-identical to each other and bit-exact
against a CPU model; a row-windowed 112x112 k3 conv is bit-exact over the whole surface
with no inter-task gap. Sweeping the control field at each width isolates the cause to
`TASK_NUMBER` alone: with the field correctly placed every control-bit value tried
(`0x0`-`0x3`, `0x5`-`0x7`) passes, and with it misplaced every non-unit task count
fails. The
RK3576-only `state_init` hook in the vendor driver is **not** needed, and neither is
`pc_task_status_offset` (`rocket` never reads `PC_TASK_STATUS`) or `pc_dma_ctrl`.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-25]

The patch is `rk3576/npu/0008` in the `rk3576-npu` profile. On an unpatched `rocket`
every register sweep on this part has to put a gap between runs, or it measures the wall
instead of the register.

The behaviour was first reported against a different open userspace (mesa/rocket +
Teflon), where a chained `task_number=N` submit produced an empty result for every
non-first task and splitting into independent single-task submits did not help; that
effort concluded the arm was per-power-session rather than per-submit.
[external, gahingwoo `MATMUL-PIPELINE-ANALYSIS.md`] Reproducing it on
our own encoder and our own submit path is what placed it in the driver rather than in
the register program. See [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).

Their analysis also settles a structural question in our favor: RK3576 matmul is the same
CNA→CBUF→CSC→CORE→DPU pipeline in fully-connected mode, with no separate GEMM unit, which
matches the RK3588 and means our matmul-as-conv framing transfers unchanged.

## SoC integration

All [source-confirmed] from the vendor kernel / RFC / BL31, per
`notes/rk3576-npu-values.md`.

- **Core bases**: npu0 `0x27700000` (cna `0x27701000`, core `0x27703000`, mmu
  `0x27702000`); npu1 **`0x27708000`** (cna `0x27709000`, core `0x2770b000`, mmu
  `0x2770a000`). MMU is at core+0x2000. NPU GRF syscon `0x26018000`.
  **Cores are 0x8000 apart, inside one 64 KB `RKNN TOP` window** — not the RK3588's
  0x10000 per-core stride. The upstream RFC device tree places core 1 at `0x27710000`,
  which is wrong: the TRM memory map gives `RKNN TOP` 64 KB at `0x27700000` and a
  *separate* `RKNN_NSP` 64 KB block at `0x27710000` [TRM], the vendor kernel's `rknpu`
  node reads `reg = <0x27700000 0x8000>, <0x27708000 0x8000>` [source-confirmed], and
  `0x27708000` returns core 0's exact version pair on live silicon while `0x27710000`
  returns a word that changes between boots [HW sweep]. The RFC's own `rknn_mmu_1` at
  `0x2770a000` is already correct, so its core node contradicts the core+0x2000 rule.
  A driver pointed at `0x27710000` drives the NPU MCU subsystem: garbage version word,
  every job times out, and the ensuing `rocket_core_reset()` takes core 1's IOMMU down
  (`MMU_DTE_ADDR is not functioning`). Our `patches/rk3576/npu/0007` carries the fix.
- **IRQs**: GIC_SPI 247 (core0 + mmu0), 248 (core1 + mmu1); core and its MMU share the
  line.
- **Power domains**: `RK3576_PD_NPU` / `_NPUTOP` / `_NPU0` / `_NPU1` (the vendor DT
  powers two core domains from one node).
- **Clock**: the compute engine is clocked through a separate, firmware-managed (SCMI)
  domain, floating free of the CBUF bus clock — the same clock-parking shape as the
  RK3588. SCMI NPU clock = `CLK_RKNN_DSU0`. Related gates: `ACLK_RKNN0/1`,
  `HCLK_RKNN_ROOT`, `ACLK_RKNN_CBUF`, `HCLK_RKNN_CBUF`. OPP rates reach ~1 GHz
  (`1000000063` Hz); PVTPLL ring/length table present in the source.
- **Resets**: `SRST_A_RKNN0/1`, `SRST_A/H_RKNN_CBUF`, `SRST_A/H_RKNNTOP_BIU`. Note no
  `SRST_H_RKNN0/1` (unlike the RK3588).
- **IOMMU**: per-job attach/detach with an MMU reset per attach, as on the RK3588. The
  RFC carries the enabling IOMMU fixes ("take all DT clocks", "clear stale page faults
  before enabling stall") and pmdomain fixes (per-domain power-on settle delay, cycle
  power-domain resets on power-on).

## Memory system, NPU rail, and thermal envelope

Board-level machine parameters, from the Rockchip RK3576 Hardware Design Guide V1.1
[source-confirmed] plus live readings on the H96 MAX M9. These three are the platform
facts an NPU number on this part has to be read against, and each differs from the
RK3588 in a way that matters.

### DRAM is half the RK3588's, at an identical clock

| | RK3588 | RK3576 |
|---|---|---|
| Bus width | **64-bit**, 4x 16-bit channels | **32-bit**, 2x 16-bit channels |
| Max capacity | 32 GB | 16 GB (8 GB per channel) |
| Standards | LPDDR4/4X/5 | LPDDR4/4X/5 |
| PHY clock | 2112 MHz | 2112 MHz |
| Theoretical peak | ~33.8 GB/s | **~16.9 GB/s** |

The bus width is [source-confirmed] from each part's design guide (RK3576 §2.1.7.1
"a 32-bit data bus width, composed of 2 16bits DDR channels"; RK3588 "64bits data bus
width, composed of 4 16bits DDR channels"). The clock is identical on both — RK3588
`VDD_DDR_S0` is specified at 2112 MHz, and `scmi_clk_ddr` reads exactly 2112000000 Hz on
the H96 MAX M9 [live 2026-07-29]. So the ratio is exactly 2x, with no clock difference
confounding it: **an RK3576 has half the DRAM bandwidth of the part every RK3588 number
in these notes was measured on.**

Measured, on the H96 MAX M9 (8 GB, 256 MB buffers, `memcpy`, pinned to the A72 cluster,
counting read + write traffic) [live 2026-07-29]:

| Threads | Achieved |
|---|---|
| 1 | 9.0 GB/s |
| 2 | 8.7 GB/s |
| 4 | 8.3 GB/s |

Two things follow. Achieved streaming bandwidth is ~53% of theoretical peak, the usual
figure for a copy benchmark on LPDDR. And **one A72 thread already saturates the bus** —
more threads make it slightly worse, not better. That is the signature of a fully
consumed resource.

This is the mechanism behind three separately measured behaviours on this part:

- **The int32 writers' atom drop tracks DDR traffic**, going from 5.8% of row tasks quiet
  to 51.7% under a host streaming `memcpy` to DDR, while an integer spin (11.7%) and an
  L1-resident `memcpy` (9.2%) barely move it. A single host thread streaming to DDR
  consumes the whole bus, so the NPU's write DMA is contending for a resource with
  nothing left in it. The load ladder also rules thermal out as the confound: integer
  spin heats the CPU at least as hard and does not move the rate.
- **The DPU write drain is long here.** `RAW_STATUS` `DPU_0`/`DPU_1` set 82-343 us after
  `PC_DONE`, scaling with the bytes in flight, and the drain reaches 745 us on the int8
  stem at 224x224 k7 s2 — which is why `dpu_grace_us` has to default to 500 on this part.
  A bus half as wide predicts about twice the RK3588's drain for the same surface
  [inferred from the width and the clock, not separately isolated].
- **The host cube pack is now within about 2x of a straight copy.** Blocking those
  scatters into `memcpy` runs took them from per-element index calls to block moves, and
  a NEON 16x16 block took the CHW <-> NC1HWC2 transpose the rest of the way: measured, the
  conv path's feature scatter and output de-scatter run at **2.1-2.4 GB/s** against this
  part's single-thread `memcpy` ceiling of **4.4 GB/s** (`build/membench`, 256 MB). So
  what was CPU-bound index arithmetic is now bulk copying against a bus one thread already
  saturates. Threading the pack is not a lever and neither is more vectorization; the
  remaining lever is moving FEWER BYTES — which is what packing a layer's weights once
  through a handle collected (98.8 ms to 33.6), and what keeping tensors in cube layout
  between consecutive layers would collect next.

### The NPU has no separate memory rail

| | RK3588 | RK3576 |
|---|---|---|
| NPU rails | `VDD_NPU` **+ `VDD_NPU_MEM`** (405 mA, separately regulatable) | `NPU_DVDD` only |
| Peak | 0.850 V / 4.00 A / 3.40 W at 1000 MHz | 0.800 V / 4.00 A / **3.20 W** at 1000 MHz |
| Supply | DC/DC BUCK | RK806S-5 BUCK2 (5 A rating) |
| Main capacitance | > 200 uF | > 90 uF |

[source-confirmed, both design guides.] The RK3588 gives every major block its own
memory rail — `VDD_NPU_MEM`, `VDD_GPU_MEM`, `VDD_CPU_BIG0/1_MEM`, `VDD_CPU_LIT_MEM`,
`VDD_VDENC_MEM` — and notes that `VDD_NPU_MEM` "can be combined with `VDD_NPU`" only
where power consumption is not a concern. **The RK3576 has exactly one MEM rail in the
whole part, `LOGIC_MEM_DVDD`, and no NPU equivalent.** The NPU's internal memory arrays,
the CBUF among them, are supplied from `NPU_DVDD` itself.

So on this part there is no supply that could hold NPU array state up while the logic
drops. That is consistent with the wide-output poison state living in an NPU-internal
memory array rather than in a register — which the register sweep established
independently, the canary already writing both of the implicated bits at their int8
values — and with a runtime-PM power cycle being the only thing that clears it. It does not by itself
explain the cycle's ~87% success rate: runtime PM gates the on-die power domain, not the
external rail, so this is a supporting fact for that mechanism and not a measurement of
it [hypothesis].

Note also that `NPU_DVDD` peaks at ~80% of BUCK2's 5 A rating, so board headroom above
the specified operating point is thin.

### There is no NPU throttle between idle and a critical event

| | RK3588 | RK3576 |
|---|---|---|
| Package | EHS-FCBGA | EHS-FCCSP698L, 16.1 x 17.2 mm |
| θJA | 8.7 C/W | **15.84 C/W** |

[source-confirmed, both design guides; simulated on a JEDEC 2S2P board with no heat
sink, so a reference figure rather than a board number.] The smaller package sheds heat
about **1.8x worse** for the same watts.

The kernel exposes six thermal zones — `package`, `bigcore`, `littlecore`, `gpu`, `npu`,
`ddr` — so the NPU has its own sensor. But `npu-thermal` carries **one trip point, type
`critical`, at 115 C, with zero cooling devices bound to it**, and the only three cooling
devices on the part are `cpufreq-cpu0`, `cpufreq-cpu4` and `devfreq-27800000.gpu`
[live 2026-07-29]. The design guide says the same thing from the other side: its SDK
"provides temperature control strategies specifically for the CPU and GPU."

**So nothing throttles the NPU. Between its idle temperature and a 115 C critical event
there is no intermediate response.** Idle is 35-38 C across all six zones on this box,
and sustained gate runs reach 40-43 C with every cooling device at state 0, so there is
wide margin in practice — but the margin is all there is. A workload that pins the NPU
rail and the A72s together, which is what this part's host-bound library calls do, loads
two rails at once on a package that sheds heat 1.8x worse than the RK1, and the design
guide is explicit that per-module peak currents "cannot simply be summed up as the SoC's
peak current" for thermal purposes. Watch die temperature on long sweeps rather than
assuming the RK1's headroom.

## Live-hardware notes (H96 MAX M9, RK3576)

Observed on a real RK3576 box (H96 MAX M9, `rockchip,rk3576`, 8 GB, mainline Debian
forky/sid, kernel 7.1.3-1-arm64 PREEMPT_RT). The device enumerates, **jobs submit and
complete**, and with this chip's own encoder the part **computes int8 convolutions
bit-exactly**. [observed 2026-07-24/25]

### Driver and device-tree state

With `CONFIG_DRM_ACCEL=y` + `CONFIG_DRM_ACCEL_ROCKET=m` and the RK3576 NPU device-tree
nodes enabled, `rocket` probes and **`/dev/accel/accel0` comes up**
(`/sys/devices/platform/rknn/accel/accel0`, `MODALIAS=platform:rknn`), with
`27700000.npu` bound and both NPU power domains attached and runtime-suspended at idle.

**The image's in-tree `rocket` carries `patches/rk3576/npu/0001-0012` but none of
`0013`, `0014` or `0020`, so it has three memory-safety defects any `render`-group client
reaches.** One malformed submit oopses it (`0013`); a client that submits and closes
without waiting walks a use-after-free in the IOVA allocator (`0014`); and any submit the
kernel rejects for its task array frees that array twice (`0020`), corrupting the
kmalloc-16 freelist so that the fault lands in whatever allocates next. Both, the paths that reach them, and the
A/B that verifies each are in [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md) under "A rejected
submit oopses the kernel", "A BO in an in-flight job must survive the file that made it"
and "The unprivileged double free, and the crashes it was mistaken for". Until the image is rebuilt, a kernel that has taken one Oops is not a kernel to
measure on — gates run afterwards, but three Oopses take the file-close path into the
IOMMU layer and the box needs a reboot.

Both patches are in `series/rk3576-npu.toml`, which listed the series only to `0012`
until 2026-07-28: a rebuild before that would have silently left `0013` out. Check the
series file, not the directory, when asking what an image carries.

The accel device rocket registers is a synthetic `platform:rknn` aggregate with **no
`of_node`**, so the chip cannot be identified from `/sys/class/accel/accel0/device`. Read
the compatible from the driver-bound per-core device instead
(`/sys/bus/platform/drivers/rocket/27700000.npu/of_node/compatible`), or fall back to the
SoC-level `/proc/device-tree/compatible`. This is what librocketnpu's chip autodetect
does.

DT nodes as instantiated: `npu@27700000` and `npu@27708000`, both
`compatible = "rockchip,rk3576-rknn-core"`, and `iommu@27702000` / `iommu@2770a000`,
`compatible = "rockchip,rk3576-iommu", "rockchip,rk3568-iommu"`. The core 0 and MMU
addresses and the interrupts (GIC_SPI 247 / 248) are confirmed on live silicon by
`rocket` binding and computing there. **Core 1's is confirmed by reading it, not by the
node existing** — a DT unit-address is only a claim, and the RFC's was wrong (see "Core
bases"). The NPU regulator `vdd_npu_s0` is PMIC `dcdc-reg2` (750 mV nominal, 550–950 mV).

Two device-tree details are required to get there, and both are easy to get wrong:

- **List BOTH NPU power domains on core 0.** `rocket_core_init()` calls
  `devm_pm_domain_attach_list()`. A node with a *single* `power-domains` entry is
  auto-attached by the driver core first, so the explicit attach then fails with
  **`-EEXIST`** (`failed to attach NPU power domains`, probe error -17) and no
  `/dev/accel` appears. Listing two entries
  (`power-domains = <&power RK3576_PD_NPU0>, <&power RK3576_PD_NPU1>`) suppresses the
  auto-attach and the explicit attach succeeds. This matches the vendor, which powers both
  NPU domains from one node even when computing on a single core — the CBUF→CMAC read path
  is only fully powered with NPU1 up.
- **Keep core 1 and its IOMMU disabled** for single-core bring-up; core 0 holds both
  domains.

Two further gaps to be aware of on a bring-up kernel:

- **The NPU rail can have no consumer, and `npu-supply` alone will not fix it.** With
  neither `npu-supply` on the NPU node nor `domain-supply` on the power domain, nothing
  claims `vdd_npu_s0`, so the regulator core disables it as unused late in boot (`vdd_npu_s0:
  disabling` at t≈33 s); probe at t≈8 s survives only because it runs first. The trap is
  that adding `npu-supply` is inert on a stock kernel: mainline `rocket` in 7.1 has **no
  regulator support at all** (`rocket.ko` references no `regulator_*` symbol — the RK3588
  `npu-supply` handling is the out-of-tree `082-rocket-drv-npu-volt` patch), so no consumer
  ever appears and the rail still drops. What holds the rail under a stock driver is
  `regulator-always-on` on the PMIC node. Set both: `npu-supply` describes the
  relationship for a regulator-aware driver, `regulator-always-on` is what actually keeps
  the rail up. With both, `vdd_npu_s0` stays `enabled` at 750 mV past boot. [live]
- **The driver may map fewer register banks than the DT provides.** Here the DT supplies
  five banks but `/proc/iomem` shows only `pc`, `cna`, `core` mapped — the DPU / DPU-RDMA
  mapping support is a separate patch and was absent from this build. This does not block
  compute: the RK3588 `rocket` maps the same three and runs DPU work, because the DPU is
  programmed through the regcmd stream rather than by the CPU. Jobs submit and complete on
  this build with only three banks mapped.

### Block bases confirmed identical to the RK3588

The node `reg-names` are **`pc`, `cna`, `core`, `dpu`, `dpu_rdma`** at core +0x0000,
+0x1000, +0x3000, +0x4000, +0x5000. So the RK3576 block bases match the RK3588's
(PC `0x0xxx`, CNA `0x1xxx`, CORE `0x3xxx`, DPU `0x4xxx`, DPU-RDMA `0x5xxx`) exactly —
independent live confirmation that the re-pack is *within* the CNA block, not a
relocation of the blocks. [live DT]

Note the binding maps more banks than the RK3588's: the RK3588 `rocket` DT exposes only
`pc`/`cna`/`core` (`fdab0000.npu pc` / `cna` / `core` in `/proc/iomem` on an RK1). That is
a driver-binding difference — the CPU only maps what it touches directly, while DPU and
PPU registers are programmed through the regcmd stream, not MMIO. Neither chip's DT maps
a PPU bank (`0x6xxx`/`0x7xxx`), so the RK3576 node list says nothing about whether the
RK3576 has a PPU pooling engine; that must be established separately.

### Clocks, resets, power

- **Both cores take the same six clocks** (`aclk hclk npu pclk aclk_cbuf hclk_cbuf`).
  The upstream RFC device tree lists only four on core 1, and that is an omission rather
  than a hardware asymmetry: the CRU has exactly one `ACLK_RKNN_CBUF` and one
  `HCLK_RKNN_CBUF` (there is no `..._CBUF1`), the vendor kernel lists them once on an
  aggregate node covering both cores, and the RFC's own `rknn_mmu_1` and
  `power-domain@RK3576_PD_NPU1` both list them. It reads as an oversight nobody upstream
  would have hit, since the RFC ships both cores `status = "disabled"`. It is load-bearing
  because `rocket_core_init()` hard-gets all six with `devm_clk_bulk_get()` for every
  core, so core 1 fails probe with `-ENOENT: Failed to get clk 'aclk_cbuf'` until the
  node lists them. `patches/rk3576/npu/0007` adds them.
- **`reset-names` is `srst_a` only.** The RK3576 has no `SRST_H_RKNN0/1` analog, and the
  binding carries no `srst_h` slot — this closes that question, which the vendor-kernel
  extraction left unverified.
- **NPU CRU clocks are present and named as expected**: `clk_rknn_dsu0` (the SCMI NPU
  clock), `aclk_rknn0`, `aclk_rknn1`, `aclk_rknn_cbuf`, `hclk_rknn_root`, `hclk_rknn_cbuf`,
  `pclk_nputop_root`, plus `scmi_clk_npu`. Confirms the clock symbol names above.
- **Power domains**: `npu` → `nputop` → `npu0`, `npu1` (hierarchical, two compute
  domains), all `off` at idle. Confirms the 2-core structure.
- **The clock does not park.** `scmi_clk_npu` / `clk_rknn_dsu0` read ~786 MHz
  (786431952 / 786431998 Hz) with the NPU power domains off, and stay at 786 MHz with the
  domains **on** and jobs running — sampled once per second across a 200-submit loop, and
  again after it drained. So the RK3588 story (boots at 200 MHz, needs the clock patch to
  reach 600 MHz) does **not** transfer: there is no RK3576 analog of the
  `POWER_DOWN_FREQ`-style park to undo, and no clock patch is needed for the RK3576 to run
  at its boot rate. [live]
- **The NPU has no DVFS at all in this kernel.** There is no NPU `devfreq` node and no
  OPP table exposed — `/sys/class/devfreq/` carries only `27800000.gpu` (800 MHz, with a
  300-950 MHz rate list). The NPU sits at a fixed 786 MHz, and `vdd_npu_s0` sits at
  750 mV against a 550-950 mV range. [live 2026-07-29]
- **The specified operating point is 1000 MHz at 800 mV**, and the CRU's OPP data reaches
  ~1 GHz (`1000000063` Hz), so there is roughly 1.27x of clock headroom above the boot
  rate and the regulator range covers the voltage it needs [source-confirmed: RK3576
  Hardware Design Guide V1.1 peak-current table]. Reaching it means an SCMI `set_rate`
  from inside the driver, as on the RK3588 — there is no sysfs interface to it. It is
  unlikely to be the lever on this part: the submit is 22% of library wall time against
  the host's 78%, and within a submit the floor is the completion poll and the DPU drain
  rather than MAC throughput. Unmeasured.
- **The CBUF is clocked as one unit for both cores.** There is exactly one
  `aclk_rknn_cbuf` (786 MHz) and one `hclk_rknn_cbuf` (198 MHz) in the tree, with no
  per-core variant [live]. This agrees with the TRM's shared 16-bank CBUF clock gate and
  with the absence of any per-core CBUF field in the NPU GRF, and is consistent with the
  two-core corruption being contention on one shared CBUF datapath [hypothesis].
- **Power domains cycle per job.** `npu`/`nputop`/`npu0`/`npu1` are all `off` at idle and
  all read `on` while jobs are in flight, returning to `off` afterwards — the driver's
  runtime-PM path works on this SoC. [live]

### Host CPU

4× Cortex-A72 + 4× Cortex-A53 big.LITTLE (`0xd08` / `0xd03`). Host fan-out for NPU offload
should pin to the A72 cluster — the analog of the RK3588's A76 recipe.

### The dispatch spine is independent of the encoder

`librocketnpu` builds unmodified on the RK3576 (gcc 15.3 / cmake 4.3, `libdrm-dev` plus
the kernel's own `drm/rocket_accel.h`) and its fp16 matmul test submits successfully.
What a submission establishes, with the **RK3588** regcmd encoder driving RK3576 silicon:

- **The whole dispatch spine works.** `DRM_IOCTL_ROCKET_SUBMIT` returns 0, the power
  domains come up, the job runs, and `PREP_BO` on the output BO returns 0 — the completion
  fence signals, so the DPU raised its completion IRQ. No `NPU job timed out`, no IOMMU
  fault, no error in `dmesg` across hundreds of submits (M=4/K=32/N=16 and
  M=64/K=128/N=64). BO allocation, IOMMU mapping, regcmd fetch, PC execution, and the
  IRQ/fence path all behave as on the RK3588. [live]
- **The DPU writes nothing.** The output BO comes back untouched (still the 0xAA
  sentinel). Not garbage, not a partial result — no write at all. This is the predicted
  consequence of the re-packed geometry registers: the RK3588 program lands its output
  dimensions in offsets that mean something else on this part, so the output surface is
  mis-sized to nothing. [live]

The useful split: **nothing above the encoder is in doubt on the RK3576.** Compute is
gated on the per-chip geometry encoder alone — not the driver, the uAPI, the memory path,
or the interrupt path. That is what made the int8 conv encoder the whole of the bring-up
work, and it is why an operation that still emits the RK3588 encoding writes nothing at
all rather than something close.

`librocketnpu` reads the NPU's DT compatible at device open, selects this chip's profile,
and warns which paths have no RK3576 encoder yet, so that silence is diagnosable rather
than mysterious. Force a profile with `ROCKET_CHIP=<name>` for bring-up.

### State and next steps

The device enumerates, jobs submit and complete, the clock and power behavior are
characterized, and an RK3576 int8 conv encoder now exists and has been run on the part.
Thermals stay 44–49 C through submit loops (this box is limited to probing, so nothing
here is a performance measurement).

**The part computes convolutions.** With that encoder a k=3 SAME 32x32 int8 conv,
IC=OC=32, driven by a dense random weight set reproduces the CPU model bit-exactly over
the whole surface (32768/32768, reproducible), and an unpadded conv over a dense feature
ramp is bit-exact as well (28800/28800). So the CNA operand DMA, the CBUF staging, the
CSC, the CMAC, the BS bias add, the OUT_CVT requant, the output geometry and the writer
are all correctly programmed, and the weight cube is the RK3588's. The envelope reaches
IC and OC from 8 to 128, planes from 8x8 to 128x64, k1/k3/k5, stride 1 and 2, VALID and
SAME, the full signed int8 feature range, and the padded border. Its three boundaries — both
channel counts *programmed* as multiples of 32, a feature plane inside the CBUF budget,
and a resident weight slice inside what the output-channel group count allows — are
closed by construction in the emitter. The encoding, the gate, the corrections to
the published map, and what the hardware settles are in
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).

**A plane past the CBUF budget is no longer a hard stop.** The emitter cuts a conv into
row windows — each task reading a window of the input rows and writing the output rows
it supports — and the split reproduces the CPU model bit-exactly over the whole surface
at every shape tried, including 112x112 and 224x224 at ic=32, which have no single-task
plan at all. Driving it also closed the three registers that take a second value only on
a windowed program (`0x40b8`, `0x1018`, `0x1038`). Each window is its own submit, which
costs about **1.2 ms** — set by the driver's completion poll rather than by the
silicon, so a split scales linearly in windows and the planner's job is to use the fewest
that fit.

**The direct path is swept gap-free and its boundaries are closed by construction.**
With a `rocket` carrying the per-SoC `PC_TASK_CON` width the whole envelope runs in one
NPU power session with no inter-shape gap and no wall retries, which removes the doubt
attaching to every earlier result taken through the retry compensation. 102 shapes pass
in 4.6 s, 67 of them direct: the envelope, the row window as a matrix, the channel-group jump across group
count and kernel size, and the weight-slice boundary asserted in both directions — the
shapes inside it compute bit-exactly and the shapes past it are refused rather than run.
The two limits the sweep sharpened are the resident weight slice (`ic*kh*kw <= 4608`
drives every output channel; past it the trailing groups are lost one at a time) and an
API trap in the row planner, where a single-task plan can still carry a window shorter
than the plane. Both are in
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).

**Depthwise computes bit-exactly**, over 35 gate shapes: channel counts 8 to 256
including those that are not multiples of 32, kernels 1 through 7, both strides, SAME and
VALID, planes to 112x112 including odd ones, and across a row window. Its register
program came from manufactured captures at 22 channel counts, which closed four formulas
that had been fitted through the found captures' confounds — the channel field in
`0x4050`, two different channel granules, the destination surface's round-up to four, and
`0x118C`, which is `iw-1` in both halves and had read as the plane height for as long as
every capture was square.

**Register fidelity was never going to be enough, and depthwise is the case that shows
why.** A `.rknn` carries a register program; it says nothing about the memory those
registers address. Two of this path's BUFFERS are not the direct path's and neither is in
any capture: the coefficient group is 48 bytes for 8 output channels with no weight-zero-
point field, where the direct group is 64 with one, and the int8 weight cube groups
channels by 64 with two live bytes per channel per tap, where the float cube groups by 32
with one 16-bit slot. Both were read off the part. Both are silent when wrong — the wrong
coefficient group writes a correctly sized, mostly empty surface, which is the same
signature a wrong geometry register gives. The layouts and the probes that decoded them
are in [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).

One consequence reaches every caller: **the depthwise output surface stride is
`round4(ow*oh_full)` elements**, not the plane. Size the output BO and de-scatter with
`rocket_rk3576_out_surf_elems()`. Assuming the plane is invisible at 16x16 and 112x112 and
wrong at 15x18.

Two negatives worth not re-deriving. The **RK3576 TRM carries no NPU register chapter** —
Part 1 and Part 2 hold the memory map (`RKNN TOP` 64 KB at `0x27700000`, `RKNN_NSP` 64 KB
at `0x27710000` — worth knowing, since it is where a mis-addressed core 1 lands), the
interrupt table
and the CRU clock tree, and nothing on the CNA/CORE/DPU registers. Unlike the RK3588,
there is no documentary path to this encoding; the captures are the only source. And the
**`.rknn` file holds the *stored* register program**: the vendor runtime patches values
into it at load time — the addresses, and at least `0x1064` — so a register that reads 0
in a decoded capture is not thereby known to be 0 on the wire.

fp16 is a transcription now, not a sweep. Every `.rknn` on hand was int8, so its
precision fields had no oracle and were inferred from the RK3588's field packing — but a
float capture can be manufactured: `do_quantization=False` on a FLOAT ONNX makes
RKNN-Toolkit2 emit a genuine fp16 conv program for this part
(`tests/data/rk3576-vendor-capture/float/mkfloat.py`). **An fp16 convolution computes
bit-faithfully at any input-channel count, delivers every output channel, and a caller
can drive it** — `gen_conv2d_fp16_rk3576()` emits the one task the part computes exactly,
which is **16 input channels**, and `rocket_rk3576_plan_ic()` lays an arbitrary `ic` out
as that many slices, summed on the host. Anything outside the envelope is refused rather
than warned about, because a wrong `ic` writes a full, correctly sized, WRONG surface with
nothing to fault on.

**Three registers carry the float mode, and each is inert while either of the others is
wrong**: CNA `0x100C` = `bit21 | proc(fp16)<<7 | in(fp16)<<4`, DPU `0x4038` low half
`0x0092`, and DPU `0x4050` with **bit 31 clear**. With any one of them at its integer
value the contraction reads every feature surface twice and skips the odd ones. That is
why no single-register sweep found it, and two of the three are not reachable by a
single-*bit* sweep from the integer word either. Of the 139-word program only fourteen
registers differ from the integer one and only seven move with `ic` at all, which is what
made the diff readable. The float weight cube groups **both** channel axes by 16, not by
the int8 cube's 32, and an fp16 *output* needs the float narrowing at DPU `0x40B0` bit 16,
because `0x4010` bits [31:29] select an output **width** and not a conversion.

**What used to read as two independent gaps is one fact.** The DPU's output element
stride is `16/ic` words. At `ic = 8` that is two words per element — the "writer spends
four bytes on a two-byte element and only `oc/2` channels reach DDR" reading — and at
`ic = 32` two channels collide in one slot and the odd ones are lost. At `ic = 16` it is
exactly one: the surface is the plain native cube, every programmed channel present
exactly once. So the bound is a contraction width, not a writer defect, and the split
around it reproduces the model exactly at `ic` 16/32/64/128 crossed with `k` 1/3/5. It was
invisible to any probe that holds the feature uniform in one axis, which is why an
exact-looking datapath was not one. Details, the probe set and the trap list are in
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).
[source-confirmed + HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-26]

What that costs is `ic/16` submits at about **1.4-1.5 ms of NPU wall each**, flat in the
work: `k = 3` at 56x56 does nine times the multiply-accumulates of `k = 1` and takes the
same 6 ms. There is no compute term visible at these shapes — it is the per-submit
dispatch floor, the same 1.1-1.3 ms the int8 path pays ONCE for its whole `ic`. Doubling
the contraction width halved both the submit count and the NPU wall against the earlier
8-channel split. Host packing and de-scatter add a real share at a full plane, and it is
the split's too — `ic/16` weight repacks and `ic/16` de-scatters — but the host column
varies by a factor of two run to run on this thermally limited board and only the NPU
column is a measurement.
[HW, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-26]

**The first conv computes too, in fp16, so a vision stem runs.** A convolution whose
input carries four or fewer channels is not the one above at a small channel count: the
CNA reads a PACKED IMAGE straight out of DDR and expands every pixel to four lanes, so
one task contracts the whole image and there is no input-channel split to make.
`rocket_conv2d_fp16_rk3576()` takes the same row-major CHW tensor as every other entry
and owns the interleave, the lane cube, the output-channel split and the de-scatter.
Bit-exact against a CPU model over 24 gate shapes — image channels 1 through 4, kernels
1/3/5/7, both strides, SAME and VALID, planes 16x32 through **224x224**, and output
channels 16 through 96. The 224x224 stride-2 stem is the one a vision model actually
opens with; it has no single-task plan at all (6272 CBUF granules against a 6144
ceiling), so it runs through the row window, and rows and output channels split together
on the two axes. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

Three things had to be right together, and only the first was already known. The float
weight cube — four lanes per (output channel, tap), output channels interleaved by
sixteen inside a tap — was decoded from manufactured captures. The **register program is
a different one at fp16**, not the int8 program at another precision: there is no channel
fold, the CBUF entry count loses the fold's `+1` lookahead, the row stride takes the
element size and the CVT is bypassed rather than configured. And **one program delivers
thirty-two output channels and no more** — at `oc` 48, 64 and 96 exactly 32 whole
channels come back and the rest of the surface is never written, even though the program
matches the vendor's own captures at those shapes register for register. The library
splits the output channels around it, one submit per tile of 32.

**The vendor's own float value is not always the one that computes**, and this path is
where that shows. `0x501C`, the BS operand reader, reads `0x100` in every vendor float
program against the integer path's `0x710`; emitting the vendor's value makes the DPU
write nothing at all, with no fault and nothing in dmesg. Paired with the BS ALU config
those programs also carry (`0x4044 = 2`) the writer runs again and the arithmetic is
wrong on about a tenth of the surface. The vendor's float epilogue is a different BS
arrangement, and this library's coefficient group is packed for the integer one — which
is bit-exact under either ALU mode. A capture is an oracle for a program, not for a
program spliced into a different one.

**The INT8 first conv computes as well, so a QUANTIZED stem runs**, and it is the one
to use: 3.9 ms against the fp16 form's 257 ms at the same 224x224 stride-2 shape.
`rocket_conv2d_int8_rk3576()` owns the packed image, the cube, the row window, the
output-channel split, the zero-point fold and the de-scatter, and is bit-exact against a
CPU model at twenty shapes — image channels 2-4, kernels 3/5/7, both strides, planes
16x32 to 224x224 and `oc` 32 to 96, including the stem at `oc` 64 and at `k` 7.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

Its weight cube is a different object from the float one in every axis but the lanes —
one byte per weight, output channels grouped by 32, the tap row outside that group and
the tap column folded into the same `round16(4*kw)` row as the four lanes — and it was
read off the part rather than out of a capture, because a quantized ARGB capture's
weights do not survive quantization as a findable value set. It is also the one path
here with no per-program output-channel cap: one int8 program delivers 64 channels where
one float program delivers 32.

**Three geometry bounds come with it and two are silent**, so all three are refused at
the entry: a NON-ZERO left pad (`CNA_PAD_CON0` bits[15:8] alone decide whether the DPU
writes at all, and `pad_top` does not), an output width of exactly `iw/stride`, and an
output width that is also a multiple of 16. None appears in any capture, because every
captured first conv is a 3x3 stride-2 SAME convolution and satisfies all three. A fourth
is no longer a bound: ONE image channel, which the emitted program will not fetch, is
programmed as two — a zero lane against zero weights — and is bit-exact to 224x224 with
the same envelope as two. A TFLite stem does not satisfy the pad bound directly and IS
still reachable; see "A TFLite stem is reachable" below. See
[rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md) for the cube and the measurements.

**A FOURTH BOUND IS THE INPUT ZERO POINT, and it is on the BORDER alone.** At a non-zero
input zero point this path's interior stays bit-exact and its padded taps do not — the
border falls to about half exact the moment the zero point leaves zero, while the DIRECT
path with the image widened to eight channels is exact everywhere including its border at
the same zero points and against the same reference. `CNA_PAD_CON1` is live (driving it
moves the count) but no single centring of it explains the readings, so the constant's
domain on this sub-encoding is NOT decoded and the shape is refused rather than computed
wrong. Nothing that had been run was wrong: every first-conv shape in every gate carries a
zero input zero point, and a TFLite MobileNet's does too after the uint8 rebase. It took a
probe that varied the zero point on this path specifically
(`tests/rk3576_argb_pad.c`). [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

### A TFLite stem is reachable, by moving the input rather than the pad

"Can the program express a zero left pad?" is settled at the GEOMETRY, not at
`CNA_PAD_CON0`: a non-zero left pad and an output extent of `ow*stride == iw` are jointly
inconsistent with a zero lead at k3 s2, where a zero lead gives `ow = iw/2 - 1` and never
`iw/2`. The entry refuses before any register is written, and no reading of the pad
register would change that.

**But the stem is reachable anyway, and the parity argument was incomplete.** "A stride-2
sample grid's parity cannot be moved by padding" is true and does not finish the sentence:
the grid also moves when the INPUT moves, and an input shifted one pixel against a pad of
one is a shift of TWO — a whole stride. Extend the image by TFLite's trailing row and
column, feed the program that buffer starting at row 1 column 1, leave both register pads
at 1, and output `(y,x)` taps image rows `2y..2y+2` and columns `2x..2x+2`. That is
TFLite's grid exactly.

Measured at 224x224 k3 s2 oc=32: **394272 of 394272 interior outputs bit-exact** against a
reference that is TFLite's own SAME convolution over the original image. What is left is
the output's own top ROW and left COLUMN — 1.8% of the surface, whose outermost taps read
the hardware's pad constant instead of image row 0 and column 0 — and that is a host
fix-up, not a barrier. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_argb_pad.c`]

**Wired into a real graph it is a LOSS, and that inverts the reading above.** The
lowering is in `rk3576_net_gate` behind `ROCKET_RK3576_NET_TFLITE_STEM=1` and is byte-for-
byte identical to the widened direct path over all 401408 elements of the stem's output —
an A/B the gate runs whatever the default is, since the border is computed by the same
reference everything else is asserted against and a comparison against that reference
alone would say nothing about it. But interleaved at `bench 100` on a resident graph it
costs **5.5-5.8 ms against the widened path's 3.5-3.7**, taking the inference from ~30 ms
to ~31.5, even though it drops the layer from five submits to one.

The "worth about 4 ms" reading compared it to the widened path **transient** (10.6-12.2
ms), and resident weights had already collected that. Two measured costs stand between it
and a win:

- **The packed-image path has no resident handle.** Profiled, the call is 3.73 ms of
  which BO teardown alone is 1.29 (36%): it allocates and frees the image, regcmd, weight,
  coefficient and output buffers every inference. That is exactly what
  `rocket_conv2d_int8_pack_rk3576()` removes from the direct path, and it refuses at
  `ic <= 4` because the packed-image weight cube is a different object.
- **The border fix-up is 1.7-1.9 ms for 1.8% of the surface.** It is memory-bound and not
  MAC-bound — 7136 outputs, nine scattered input cache lines each — so hoisting the loop's
  bounds into registers moved it by under 0.2 ms.

Both closed it lands near 2.9 ms against 3.5, so it is a sub-millisecond lever on a graph,
not the several-millisecond one it was taken for. Kept, gated and exact; off by default.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_net_gate.c`, 2026-07-29]

**What broke it open was submitting a vendor capture verbatim.** Our emitter reproduced
twelve int8 ARGB captures register for register and still wrote nothing at every geometry
tried, and a leave-one-out over the nineteen-register delta against the working fp16
program revived none of it — which read as a dead datapath. Taking the captured op stream
out of the golden table, patching only the five address registers and submitting it
(`tests/rk3576_fc_vendor.c`) showed the vendor's own program writing its whole surface.
The mode worked; the extrapolation away from the captured geometry did not, and walking
back from that geometry one axis at a time is where all four bounds came from.

**The CNA converter's offset is inert.** The ARGB register table lists `0x1054`-`0x105C`
as the uint8 zero point the CVT subtracts, which is what the datapath's description says
— but every ARGB capture is zero point 0, so those registers were transcribed and never
exercised. Driven on the part they do nothing: an image written at `raw = s + (zp+0x80)`
comes back with the raw byte read as a signed int8 and no subtraction at all, `0x80`
reading -128 and `0xFF` reading -1. So the packed image is plain two's complement and the
input zero point folds into the coefficient group's `A` term exactly as the direct path
folds it. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

**The fp16 first conv poisons its own next submit**, and that is most of its wall time.
A second submit of the same program comes back with the surface never written; the write
rate against the inter-submit gap is 1/20 at 0 and 20 ms, 8/20 at 60 ms and 20/20 from
100 ms. It is not a property of the first-conv sub-encoding — it is the one hazard the
whole part carries on any wide output, and the section below is where that is settled.
**The int8 first conv has no such hazard**: 20 of 20 submits write at a zero gap.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

## The poisoning is one hazard, and its cost is a system setting

**Any program whose DPU output element is wider than one byte leaves the NEXT submit —
of any kind, across processes — completing normally and writing nothing.** One byte never
carries it. An A-poisons-B matrix over seven program kinds, each pair measured with a
canary that is known to write, splits them exactly: the fp16 first conv, the fp16 direct
conv, and both int32 writers poison every one of the seven, while the int8 first conv,
the int8 direct conv and the int8 depthwise poison none of them. Every A wrote its own
surface in every cell, so no cell is a dead program reading as a clean one.
`tests/rk3576_poison_probe.c` is the instrument, and its design is the point: asking
whether the program under test wrote cannot tell a DEAD program from a POISONED one, so
it scores the canary instead and lets the program under test compute nonsense.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

**A sacrificial submit does not absorb it.** After one int32 job, six consecutive int8
jobs at a zero gap all wrote nothing. The state persists until the power domain cycles,
so the guard has to be an idle rather than a cheap dummy job.

**The two paths poison through DIFFERENT register conditions.** Both were found by
delta debugging (`rk3576_poison_probe heal`) over the register delta between a poisoning
program and its clean counterpart at the same geometry: apply the whole delta, confirm it
stops the poisoning, then bisect. A leave-one-out asks whether any ONE register is
sufficient, and every negative it returns is equally consistent with a condition of two —
which is why the earlier one-at-a-time sweeps found nothing on the fp16 path.

- **The int32 writers: one register.** `gen_conv2d_int8_rk3576_i32out()` differs from the
  int8 direct conv in four registers, and DPU `0x4010` (`out<<29 | in<<26 | proc`) alone
  carries it — restoring it stops the poisoning with the program still writing. Forced
  onto the int8 direct conv, `out` values 1-3 are clean and 4-5 poison.
- **The fp16 path: exactly two bits, and neither alone.** The fp16 direct conv writes the
  same 96 registers as the int8 one — no register is exclusive to either — and 11 values
  differ. Minimised, the condition is DPU **`0x4038` and `0x4050` together**: each
  restored alone leaves the canary at 0/8, both restored take it to 8/8 with the program
  still writing. An exhaustive truth table over the six differing bits of those two
  registers narrows it further to **`0x4038[4]` and `0x4050[17]`**: every combination that
  clears both is clean, every combination that leaves either set poisons, and no other
  bit matters. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

**Those two bits are the float arithmetic itself, so there is no fp16 encoding that does
not poison.** `0x4038[4]` is part of the low half that must read `0x92` rather than the
direct path's `0x80`, and `0x4050[17]` is the high-half field that reads 2 at float and 1
at int8. With both cleared the fp16 direct conv still writes its surface and stops
poisoning — and every one of the five fp16 direct-conv shapes that reproduces a CPU model
exactly now computes wrong. The hazard cannot be encoded away on this path; it can only
be paid for.

**The state does not live in the DPU register file.** The nine DPU registers neither
program writes (`0x4000`, `0x4008`, `0x4040`, `0x4054`, `0x4064`, `0x4068`, `0x4098`,
`0x40a0`, `0x40c4`) written explicitly by the next job leave it poisoned; the canary
already writes `0x4038` and `0x4050` at their int8 values and is poisoned anyway. Whatever
carries it is internal and is reset by the power domain collapsing, not by a register
write.

**Nothing absorbs it, on any path.** One poisoning job followed by six submits, all four
poisoning program kinds, every position 0 of 6. The guard has to be an idle.

**A DEAD program can poison.** The four truth-table rows that drive `0x4038`'s low half to
`0x80` while the rest of the program stays float write nothing themselves and still leave
the canary at 0/6. "It wrote nothing" is not a proxy for "it was harmless", which is the
trap in any register sweep here that scores the program under test.

**What IS settled is the price.** The idle that clears it tracks
`power/autosuspend_delay_ms`, and the law is TWICE the delay: over a 5x7 sweep of delay
against gap, the gap at which a chain of wide-output submits first reaches 20 of 20 is
~100 ms at a 50 ms delay, ~40 at 20, ~20 at 10, ~10 at 5 and ~5 at 1.

**Lowering that delay system-wide is not free**, though a back-to-back chain cannot see
why: a resume costs about 230 us, and lowering the delay makes every job whose inter-job
gap falls between the new delay and the old one pay it. At a 16.7 ms gap — one 60 Hz frame,
which is the pipeline mainline's 50 ms was chosen for — a submit goes from 1124 us to 1372.
At a zero gap the two are identical, because the timer never fires.

**So the library drives the power cycle instead of waiting for it.**
`rocket_rk3576_power_idle()` writes a zero delay, polls `runtime_status` until it reads
`suspended`, and puts the caller's delay back; the guard costs one real cycle rather than
a worst-case idle, and the steady-state policy every other consumer of the NPU sees is
unchanged. It needs write access to that sysfs file and falls back to the plain idle
without it (`ROCKET_RK3576_PM_KICK=0` turns it off). At the **stock 50 ms delay** and with
every gate still bit-exact, the fp16 first conv's 224x224 k3 s2 runs in **13.75 ms against
226**, its whole gate group in 2.3-15.6 ms against 107-334, and the int32 matmul gate in
9.9 s against 16.2 — better than lowering the global delay to 1 ms, which reached only
21.6 ms and taxed every gapped workload to get there. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

Four things the table says, at this operating point rather than as properties of the
silicon:

- **The submit is a floor of about 0.44 ms, and the submit-bound rows track it.** The
  rows that moved most when the completion poll went from 500 us on `PC_DONE` to 50 us on
  the DPU bit are the ones with least work per submit — `conv 32->64 k1 56x56` 3.12 ->
  2.01, `depthwise 256 k3 14x14` 1.42 -> 0.96 — while the rows dominated by host packing
  or by a power cycle barely move.
- **Depthwise wins by 2.1-3.7x**, and against a threaded CPU kernel it would be close.
  That much is arithmetic rather than encoding: a depthwise conv has `ic` times fewer
  MACs than the direct conv at the same shape, so the same submit floor buys `ic` times
  less. Batching several depthwise layers into one submit is the lever the encoding
  would have to supply.
- **The matmul's axis is N, and the planner already spends it.** At M=32 the planner
  puts K=N=1024 in a SINGLE submit — the largest N tile the part will take — so the
  earlier reading that the matmul was dispatch-bound at low M was wrong. What it was
  bound by was the host cube scatter, below.
- **The quantized stem is still the stem to run**, but the gap is 3.2 ms against 13.7
  rather than against 226. What closed it was driving the power cycle the fp16 path's
  guard waits for, not arithmetic — the residue is one real cycle per call, and the fp16
  path pays the `dpu_grace_us` blind settle on top because it raises no DPU completion.

**The host cube scatter was most of the wall, not the submit.** The entries pack
row-major tensors into this part's cubes and de-scatter the results back, and doing that
an element at a time through the index functions costs a divide, a modulo and a call per
byte — which at K=N=1024 is a million of them, and was twelve of that matmul's thirteen
milliseconds. Three of those scatters are not scatters at all once the index is written
out: at `kh=kw=1`, `weight_conv_int8()` puts thirty-two consecutive contraction elements
at thirty-two consecutive bytes on both sides, and `feature_data()` at C2=16 does the
same for sixteen, so the matmul's feature pack, weight pack and output de-scatter are
blocked memcpys. The convolution entries stay a real transpose — CHW against NC1HWC2 —
and keep a strided store, but the per-element division hoists out to one pair per
channel. Everything above is measured after that change; before it the matmul tied the
CPU reference at M=32 (1.0x), depthwise ran 1.4-1.6x, and the 1x1 convolutions 2.2x.
[HW, H96 MAX M9]

**And the convolution entries' weight side was still per-element after that.** Running a
whole graph, not a shape, is what showed it — `ROCKET_RK3576_INT8_PROF=1` splits a call
into its operand packs, its submits and its de-scatter, and on the layers that cost most
the submit was 1-3% of the wall while two host loops were 90%. Both are functions of the
WEIGHTS alone, and both were walking a million elements through index arithmetic: the
per-output-channel filter sums, which are a contiguous scan written as a triple-nested
index expression, and the weight cube scatter, whose `taps == 1` case is thirty-two
contiguous source bytes landing on thirty-two contiguous cube bytes and so a run of block
moves rather than a run of byte stores. Straightening both took MobileNetV1's graph from
146 ms to 115 ms with every layer still bit-exact, and the 1x1 convolution rows of the
table above from 5.6x/15.7x to 5.3x/21.7x. [HW, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_net_gate.c`]

**A locality rewrite of the FEATURE scatter is a measured negative.** Packing pixel-outer
and channel-inner fills each C2-byte atom in one go and touches every destination line
once instead of once per channel, which is the better trade on paper and was not on the
part: it needs C2 concurrent read streams, and on the graph it cost the depthwise layers
more than it bought the 1x1 ones (layer 3's feature pack 3.2 -> 5.3 ms). The scatter stays
channel-outer — one sequential read stream and a strided store.

**A matmul computes on this part, at int8.** `rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576()` is the entry;
`tests/rk3576_matmul_gate.c` holds it against a CPU model over 33 shapes, including M
with no even divisor, K and N off the round grid, several N tiles, a row-windowed M, the
two alignment refusals, and eleven that gate the int32 output exactly. Nothing about the register program was new — a matmul is a 1x1
convolution and this part already computed those bit-exactly — so what the work was is
the userspace layer around one: the operand scatter into this part's cubes, the output
de-scatter, the tiling planner, and the per-chip dispatch point.

Three of its properties are the RK3588's answers inverted, and the last one is the reason
the entry is separate rather than a routing behind `rocket_matmul_int8()`:

- **int8 is the matmul precision here.** One int8 task takes `ic*kh*kw` up to 4608, so a
  K of 4608 lands in a **single submit**; one fp16 task contracts exactly sixteen input
  channels, so the same K costs 288. Run on one shape at both precisions, fp16 is 30x
  slower at K=512 and 300x slower at K=4608. On the RK3588 fp16 wins and int8 buys only
  RAM.
- **The M axis carries no constraint.** M = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 17 and 31 are each bit-exact,
  and so is every factorization of M into a plane — a single row of pixels, a single
  column, and everything between. On the RK3588 rows are the conv's spatial height,
  height < 4 mis-computes, `M%4` is the real bound, and software pads `M==1` to 4.
- **The output is int8, through the DPU's requant**, where the RK3588's int8 matmul
  writes raw int32 and sums K partials on the host. Inside one task's contraction that is
  the fast path and the requant is the DPU's own. Past it, K is split through the
  int32-output entry below and the requant moves to the host — the boundary is invisible
  in the result and expensive in the cost.

**Throughput is MACs per submit and nothing else.** A submit costs about 0.44 ms whatever
it carries, and two of the three axes are already capped — `M*K` by the feature budget at
262144 int8 elements, K by the resident weight slice at 4608 — which leaves N as the only
axis worth spending. Measured at the feature-budget cap it rises almost linearly with N:
12 GOP/s at N=32, 47 at 128, 367 at 1024, and about 1.0 TOP/s at N=2560. Two bounds stop
it, both of which lose the trailing output channels silently, with a full correctly sized
surface and a clean prefix: **2944 output channels** computes and 3072 is intermittent,
and independently a **6 MiB weight cube** computes where 6.75 MiB does not. Both are now
refusals in the CBUF planner rather than hopes, and the default tile of 2048 sits far
enough inside that they are guards. No convolution shape reaches either — they bite only
at the output widths a matmul drives.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-27]

**The DPU emits its 32-bit accumulator on an integer program, and that is the K split.**
`gen_conv2d_int8_rk3576_i32out()` takes `precision_int32` in DPU `0x4010`'s output-width
field and pins OUT_CVT to exact unity, and the words that come back are genuine int32
accumulators on the RK3588's int32 cube map —
`(c/4)*ow*oh_full*4 + 4*(y*ow+x) + c%4`, decoded by driving operands whose every
accumulator is distinct so that each one names exactly one word.

The writer's budget is **one 16-byte atom per (16-channel block, pixel)** whatever the
OUTPUT element width is, so at four bytes an element it delivers **the first eight output
channels of every thirty-two** and touches nothing else. The way round it is the weight
cube rather than a register: program four times the output channels, put real channel `n`
at programmed channel `32*(n/8) + n%8`, and the surface that comes back is the plain
int32 cube with every channel present.

**What the budget is a function of is the DPU's own operand width**, `PROC_PRECISION`,
not the output element's — and widening `0x4010[2:0]` from int8 to int32 makes it two
atoms. The delivered set becomes the first eight of every SIXTEEN, so a real channel
costs two programmed ones instead of four, the arithmetic is bit-identical, and the CNA
and CORE programs are byte-unchanged. Two atoms is the ceiling: int16, fp16 and bfloat16
reach it too, the float ones destroying the arithmetic on the way, and int4 and int8 do
not. `gen_conv2d_int8_rk3576_i32out_wide()` emits it and
`rocket_rk3576_i32_wide_word()` addresses it — the map is a stream `s = 2*p + j` cut into
runs of `ow*oh_full` atoms inside 32-channel super-groups, decoded across `oc` 8 to 192
and pixel counts 4 to 33. It carries two bounds that fail silently: `oc` must be a
multiple of 32, and the surface must be short. The height rule the library enforces —
`oh_full * oc < 4096` — is a SAFE CAP and not the boundary: swept with the cap lifted, the
map is exact well past it at `oc` 32, 64, 96 and 256, and wrong INSIDE it at `oc` 128. The
plane width is not in it, and the failures are neither monotone in height nor repairable
by the writer's own redo. See `chips/rk3576-regcmd.md` and
`tests/rk3576_i32_height_bound.c`.

`rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576_i32()` drives the narrow writer by default and the wide one
under `ROCKET_RK3576_I32_OC_MULT=2`. Both are bit-exact against a CPU int32 model at K up
to 16384, and `rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576()` routes a K past one contraction through it.
What the multiplier costs is the N tile, and so submits: measured 4 submits down to 2 at
M=64 K=1024 N=2048, and 8 down to 4 at M=32 K=1024 N=4096; below the tile cap the two
cost the same. The wide one is opt-in because it **intermittently emits zeros**: on
about one run in ten of the shape that shows it most, a contiguous block of one
super-group's stream comes back zero while the rest of the surface is exact. It does not
drop writes — stamp the surface with a sentinel first and the bad atoms hold zero rather
than the sentinel, so the writer reaches them and the data is wrong; on a fresh BO, which
arrives zeroed, the two readings are indistinguishable. It is not the poisoning either,
being indifferent to the idle ahead of it. The corruption is contiguous in the writer's
**emission** order and in no other, sits inside a single super-group, and always ends two
emissions short of that group's last, so it is detected and the row task is redone —
0 failures over 40 runs of the worst shape, 3 of them redoing a task, with no idle needed
in front of the redo.

Two traps come with it. **An i32out job poisons the next submit** — of any kind, across
processes, because the state lives in the NPU and not in the fd — which then completes
normally in the usual per-submit time and writes nothing. This is not a property of the int32
writer: it is the part's one wide-output hazard, and every fp16 program carries it too.
The scope, the two negatives, and the law the guard is sized by are settled above under
"The poisoning is one hazard". Check that a job WROTE per task and not per tile: one
poisoned submit among a row-split tile's leaves its own rows empty while its siblings are
full. And **an output buffer must not be zeroed from the CPU before a submit**: the dirty
cache lines race the DPU's DMA and the writeback lands on top of the result. The int32
sweep also wedged the IOMMU once, reboot-only; the mechanism is **not** established, and
the measured extent stays inside the allocated BO, so an overrun is not the explanation
the data supports. Details in [rk3576-regcmd.md](rk3576-regcmd.md).
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-27]

Of the machine parameters, the CBUF is swept — the budget, the data/weight split, and the
field that trades between them — and the matmul tile cap and weight N-group are measured
against the matmul above. Only `kgroup_4b` remains carried over, and nothing reads it.

**The op library reaches this part.** Convolution is behind
`rocket_conv2d_int8_rk3576()`, `rocket_conv2d_dw_int8_rk3576()` and
`rocket_conv2d_fp16_rk3576()`, which take row-major tensors and own the cubes, the row
plan, the output-channel split, the de-scatter and the submit-loop retries. Where the
public entry's semantics are the ones the part computes it dispatches there and a caller
writes nothing chip-specific: `rocket_conv2d_dw_int8()` and `rocket_conv2d_fp16()` both
do. `rocket_conv2d_int8()` does not — it writes a raw int32 accumulator where this part's
direct int8 datapath requantizes on chip — so it keeps refusing and names the entry that
matches, exactly as `rocket_matmul_int8()` does. `tests/rk3576_conv_lib_gate.c` drives
the emitter gate's own shape table through the library: 111 pass bit-exact, 1 refuses.

Zero points are folded exactly and gated for the first time. The input's correction is
`-in_zp*sum(w)` and needs no border special case, because the emitter programs the pad
constant to the input zero point and a pad tap's true value is therefore zero; the
weight's rides in the coefficient group's `B`, which the DPU ADDS, so it is programmed
negated; the output's becomes the `OUT_CVT` offset. Depthwise takes the input and output
ones and refuses a weight zero point, whose group has no `B` field.

**A layer's weights are packed once through a handle**, which is where most of a graph's
wall went — see the MobileNet section for the 2.94x. `rocket_conv2d_int8_pack_rk3576()`
takes the weights, the bias and the quant contract and returns one;
`rocket_conv2d_int8_prepacked_rk3576()` runs it per inference. One handle covers all three
per-call entries: `desc.depthwise` picks direct or depthwise, and a non-NULL `w_scale_oc`
picks the per-axis requant. **Three things it must freeze, and none is bookkeeping.** The
`fd`, because a BO belongs to the file that created it and an IOVA is per-fd — a handle run
on another fd would hand the NPU addresses that mean nothing there and the submit would
still succeed, so a foreign fd is refused. The output-channel TILE, because the weight
cube's group count follows the tile rather than the layer, and on the per-axis path that
tile is an accuracy choice made from the weights and the scales. And the quant contract,
because both zero points and all three scales are folded into the coefficient group at pack
time. The handle also holds the scratch whose size follows the frozen geometry — the feature
cube, the regcmd buffer, each tile's output surface — so an inference allocates no BO at
all, which turned out to matter more than the arithmetic did.

Reusing an output surface across inferences is safe on a part where "did this task write
anything" is a real check, because the sentinel is re-stamped before every submit; with the
sentinel off there is no check to mislead. Reusing the feature cube skips its zeroing memset
from the second call on, which is sound because the channels past `ic` are the cube's zero
padding and nothing writes them again.

The gate is not wall time: `ROCKET_LG_RESIDENT=1` runs 173 shapes of the library table
through pack plus **two** prepacked calls and compares to the transient output element for
element — two, because the second call is the first one to reuse a held cube and a held
surface, and one would not notice a stale byte. 0 differ.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_conv_lib_gate.c`]

**The library's envelope is wider than the emitter's, and by a decoded amount.** The
weight-slice cap is a bound on one PROGRAM, keyed on the output-channel groups that
program drives; splitting the output channels lowers the group count and so raises the
slice the part accepts. Fourteen shapes the emitter refuses compute through the library,
including `ic=576 k=3` and `ic=5184 k=1` at four tiles of 32 channels. The rule
`ic*kh*kw <= 4608` is the single-program one; through the library it is the CBUF pool at
one group, about `ic*kh*kw <= 5600`. One refusal survives — `ic=256 k=5`, at 200 KiB — and
it is the one whose only recourse is an ic split the on-chip requant forecloses.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-27]

**The per-submit floor is the driver's completion poll, and it is 439 us.** `PC_DONE` is
not routable to the GIC here, so `patches/rk3576/npu/0006` polls it on an hrtimer and
`0009` makes the period `rocket.poll_interval_us`. Measured through `rk3576_submit_floor`
(an 8x64x32 int8 matmul, n=300), the period at 50 us is **439 us/submit against 1654 at
the stock 1 ms — 3.77x**, and 2.42x against the 500 us the period sat at before. That is
every submit-bound workload, which on this part is most of them. Do not measure the floor
with a gate's total wall time; the conv gate is dominated by host packing and its CPU
model and reads flat at every period.

**What made the period safe to shorten is retiring on the DPU's own completion.**
`PC_DONE` means the program counter finished issuing, not that the DPU's write DMA has
drained, and the driver both tears the IOMMU mapping down and signals the fence as soon as
it sees it. The IOMMU half is fixed by `patches/rk3576/npu/0011` — the per-job detach
faults writes still in flight, leaving a write page fault (`RK_MMU_STATUS 0x2b`) that
blocks the next `enable_stall` — which takes stall timeouts and NPU DMA errors to zero at
every period. The visibility half is fixed by `0012`: `RAW_STATUS` bits `DPU_0`/`DPU_1`,
what the RK3588 takes as its completion interrupt, set **82-343 us after `PC_DONE`** on
this part, scaling with the bytes in flight, and requiring them retires the job when the
writes have landed. On `PC_DONE` the int8 matmul gate is 42/43 at a 250 us period and
36/43 at 50 us; on the DPU bit it is 43/43 at both, and the whole gate set is clean.

**A task whose DPU output element is wider than one byte raises no DPU completion at
all** — the int32 writer and fp16 output stop at `RAW_STATUS = 0x30000055`, CNA and CORE
done and nothing further for milliseconds — so the wait is bounded by
`rocket.dpu_grace_us` and those retire on `PC_DONE` as before. The grace is load-bearing:
they fail at 100 us and pass from 250 up, the same threshold a blind settle needed. The
default is 500 us. This is the same set of programs that carries the poisoning, but the
two are separate hazards — the poison probe's `scope` map is unchanged under `0012`.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-28]

**Every wide-output submit pays that grace in full, so it is the fp16 path's largest
single cost.** An fp16 convolution is `ic/16` such submits and takes no poisoning retries
at all, so a slice is the poll floor plus the whole blind settle: `ic=128` at 28x28 k3
runs a median 15.89 ms over 8 slices at the 500 us default and 13.29 at 250, five runs
each with disjoint ranges — 300 us of a 250 us cut, landing once per slice. **Three
consecutive full gate suites pass at 250** (each 102 conv, 156+7 conv-library, 43 matmul
on each int32 writer, 15 refusals, the host regcmd gate), which covers the wide-output
programs the grace exists for. The default has NOT been moved, because 250 is the
measured cliff edge rather than a margin above it — the value to ship is between 250 and
500, and picking it wants the int32 sweeps run at that value rather than at this one.
[HW sweep, measured 2026-07-28, `tests/rk3576_fp16_split_cost.c`]

**A per-job `rocket_core_reset()` does not clear the int32 poisoning; it takes the IOMMU
down** (`MMU_DTE_ADDR is not functioning`), drops the matmul gate from 43 shapes to 2, and
leaves the part wedged. The driver's own timeout path resets inside a scheduler stop and an
IOMMU detach/re-attach, and it is that surrounding re-init a bare call is missing; the
vendor `rknpu` driver never resets per job either.

**A half-started job pins the NPU `active`, which then breaks the int32 path and nothing
else** — fixed by `patches/rk3576/npu/0010`, and present in mainline `rocket` on the
RK3588 as well. `rocket_job_run()`'s two early returns leave both a runtime-PM reference
nothing will drop and a fence nothing will signal, and each pins the device on its own:
the usage counter never returns to zero, and the scheduler never takes back the job's
credit, which `rocket_device_runtime_suspend()` waits on. The usage-counter half lives on
the platform device, so `rmmod`/`insmod` does not clear it. The part then comes back
computing convolutions bit-exactly — 102/102 and 111/1 — while every i32 matmul shape
writes nothing, because the poisoning's clear is the power domain cycling and the domain
no longer cycles. It reads as a bug in the int32 writer and it is a machine state. Without
0010 it is also self-sustaining: one failed attach times out, the timeout path's core reset
takes the IOMMU down, and the next attach fails for real — one injected failure produced
seven and leaked seven references. Check `power/runtime_status` before believing any int32
result.
[HW, H96 MAX M9, measured 2026-07-27]

**The kernel build loop is not blocked.** The board's `linux-headers` package is a full
kbuild tree with `Makefile`, `Module.symvers`, `arch`, `include` and `scripts`, so the
module builds ON the board: take pristine v7.1 `drivers/accel/rocket/`, apply 0006 and
0008 (the only two in the series that touch that directory — the rest patch pmdomain, the
IOMMU and the DT, which are built in), give it an `obj-m` Makefile and `make -C
/lib/modules/$(uname -r)/build M=$PWD modules`. The compiler differs from the kernel's and
warns; the module loads and both conv gates and the matmul gate pass on it unchanged.

## Quantization

The RK3576 vendor path uses **per-output-channel** weight scales; Mesa's single global
scale saturates most channels. [source] This is the same per-channel requant story as
the RK3588, so the on-chip requant work (per-OC `OUT_CVT` MUL with a single global
SHIFT) transfers; see [../encodings/out-cvt-converter.md](../encodings/out-cvt-converter.md).

## The DPU LUT: the input map, and the register that hides it

A nonlinear activation on this part is two programs — a DPU-only one that loads two
513-entry tables through an auto-incrementing window, and an ordinary convolution with
the LUT enabled. `gen_lut_load_rk3576()` emits the first; the second is the direct int8
convolution plus a short register set. The load and the use want to be ONE job, because
the table lives in the LUT RAM and a separate submit can take a runtime-PM cycle.

**The input map is `index = (value - LE_START) / 2^sel`**, the `0x00020000` table
covering `[LE_START, 0]` and the `0x00030000` one `[0, LO_END)` at 512 intervals each,
with all three of `LE_START` (`0x4110`-`0x411C`, four lanes), `LO_END`
(`0x4140`-`0x414C`) and the index step (`0x410C`) live and programmable. The hardware
interpolates linearly between entries at the full resolution of the step, and the domain
is half-open at the top: `value == LO_END` takes the overflow clamp. Bit-explained
against a CPU model at four spans, 8192 samples each, max one output count
(`tests/rk3576_lut_probe.c`). [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

**The value the LUT sees is the BS stage's output** — `(acc + A[oc]) * C[oc]` — so a
caller places each output channel's range inside the window with the same per-channel
`C` a per-axis quantization already uses. That is what the vendor does, which is why it
never moves the window, and why at fp16 — where that scale rides the coefficient buffer
rather than a register — sigmoid and tanh emit register-identical programs with tables
spanning 6.25 and 3.02 input units.

**BN_CFG `0x4060` is the trap.** Every vendor activation carries `0x20` there against
`0x903` in its own bare-conv control, and transcribing it pins the LUT at the table join
for every input across twenty-two binades — while the readout still tracks the table
faithfully as the table is changed, so it reads as a working LUT with an input map that
is nowhere in the registers. The BN stage is then multiplying by an operand register no
vendor program writes, on a part whose register file is not cleared between jobs. Left
at the generator's own `0x903` the stage is bypassed and the map appears. The LUT itself
is the **EW** stage: `EW_LUT_BYPASS` is bit 7 of `0x407C`, the RK3588's field layout at
a moved offset.

### The op: an int8 activation that is exact at every input

`rocket_act_int8_rk3576()` is the entry point over that map — sigmoid, tanh, swish,
hardswish, hardsigmoid and elu over a row-major `[C][H][W]` int8 tensor with a per-tensor
quantization on both sides. `exp`, `gelu` and `sqrt` are deliberately absent: no vendor
capture emits a 513-entry table for any of the three, so they lower some other way and a
table for them would be an invention.

**It is exact for every int8 input rather than an interpolation of one.** The op lowers
onto a depthwise 1x1 identity convolution whose weight is the LUT's own index step,
`2^sel`, which puts input value `q` at table index `q - in_zp + 512` EXACTLY — so each of
the 256 possible inputs reads its own entry and the hardware's interpolation never runs.
An identity weight of 1 would have put all 256 inputs inside eight index intervals. The
per-output-channel `C` could express the same scaling and is not needed for this op: the
coefficient `A` fold already carries `-in_zp*sum_w`, so the input zero point comes out
right with no special case, and `C` stays free for a fused conv->activation where the
accumulator range differs per channel.

**Depthwise, and it has to be.** A 1x1 DIRECT identity needs an `oc x ic` weight matrix,
which is quadratic in the channel count and past the emitter's `ic*kh*kw <= 4608` weight
slice rule at any real width. Depthwise binds output channel `c` to input channel `c`, so
the identity is one weight per channel and the layer is one task whatever the width.

**The table's two scales.** A datapath value `v` means the real number
`in_scale * v / 2^sel`, and an entry is read in units of `out_scale * gain` where `gain`
is the OUT_CVT pair the emitter will actually program — read back from
`rocket_rk3576_requant_params()` rather than re-derived, because a table built against a
slightly different gain is a curve that is quietly off everywhere. The conv scale is held
at 1/128, the finest gain whose int16 entry still spans the whole int8 output range a
zero point can ask for (128 * 255 = 32640).

The two programs go out as ONE job, which needed a third submit layout in the conv driver
beside "one submit per row task" and "one chained kick": a multi-descriptor job, because
the chain rewrite links programs inside one BO and the table load is a separate one.
Chaining is declined for a LUT call and says so; it costs the single-kick saving, not
correctness.

6 kinds x 5 quantizations x 6 shapes, dense over all 256 byte values: **180 of 180
bit-exact against a CPU model of the chip's own arithmetic**, and at most **one count**
from an exact `f()` anywhere (`tests/rk3576_act_gate.c`). [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9]

### The activation fuses into a convolution's epilogue

The standalone op invents a convolution to reach the LUT. A real one already has one and
the LUT sits in the EW stage of the same program, so `rocket_conv2d_int8_act_rk3576()`
costs only the table-load task: the activation is free.

**It is not the same arithmetic as conv-then-activation, and it is the better one.** The
standalone op reads the convolution's REQUANTIZED int8 output, so its input alphabet is
256 values; the fused form reads the ACCUMULATOR, before any rounding. What it gives up is
exactness — the standalone form puts each of its 256 inputs on its own table entry, and a
real accumulator lands between entries, so the hardware's linear interpolation runs.
**Measured over 28 cases (six kinds, direct and depthwise, three quantizations): at most
ONE count from an exact `f()`**, with the pre-activation inside the curved region rather
than on a saturating tail. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_act_gate.c fused`]

**Nothing about the conv entries' contract has to change**, which is the opposite of what
pinning the standalone op's scale at 1/128 suggested. Three quantities follow from the
three scales a caller already passes:

- **The value unit.** With a per-tensor requant the BS multiplier `C` is unity, so the
  value the LUT sees is the accumulator itself — `A` included, which is where the bias and
  the zero-point fold already are — and one count of it means `in_scale*w_scale`.
- **The entry unit.** OUT_CVT scales the table's output by the gain the emitter derives
  from `in_scale*w_scale/out_scale`, so an entry is read in units of `out_scale*gain`. The
  standalone op has to PIN its conv scale to make that well defined; a real conv has the
  model's own three.
- **The step.** The window is the symmetric `[-512*2^sel, 512*2^sel)`, so `sel` is the
  smallest whose real half-span covers the domain the activation still varies over. Past
  it the LUT returns its endpoint clamp — the right answer for a SATURATING kind, and for
  one that keeps growing the bound is where the int8 OUTPUT saturates instead, since
  `f(x)` is `x` by then.

**Two bounds are refused rather than computed wrong**, and both name `in_scale*w_scale` as
the quantity to change: a value unit too fine for the table's peak to fit an int16 (an
unbounded kind over a fine unit — swish and elu at `in_scale*w_scale = 1.6e-4` need 49151
entries and the table saturates at 32767, which would give a flat-topped curve), and a
domain no `sel` reaches.

**Per-output-channel `C` is the lever this leaves open.** It divides the value unit per
channel, which is how a fused conv whose channels have very different accumulator ranges
puts each inside `[LE_START, LO_END)` — and `(acc + A)*C` saturates at int32, a bound to
plan inside. The per-tensor form above needs none of it.

TWO REFERENCE TRAPS, both of which read as hardware faults. **A liveness check against the
same conv WITHOUT the LUT has to allow a rounding**: the two surfaces differing by one
count is the requant's tie-to-even against a reference that rounds half away from zero,
not the activation doing something. And **ELU under an output quantization with no
negative range IS the identity clamped at zero**, which the DPU's int8 saturation already
computes — so on that quantization the fused and plain surfaces are legitimately
identical and a differs-from-plain assertion fires on a working LUT.

## The precision fork: what the op library should be founded on here

The RK3588's composed op library — the norms, softmax, the gated FFN, attention, the
ViT/Whisper encoder blocks — is built on the **fp16 matmul**. That foundation does not
port to this part, and the choice of what to put in its place is a decision rather than a
port.

**fp16 is not a matmul precision here.** One int8 task contracts `ic*kh*kw <= 4608` and one
fp16 task contracts exactly sixteen input channels, so the same K costs one submit against
288, and the measured ratio runs 30x to 299x over ordinary shapes. Anything that lowers to
an fp16 GEMM inherits that. This is the RK3588's answer inverted, and it is a machine
parameter, not arithmetic.

**int8 carries the GEMMs but not the reductions.** `rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576()` is fast
and bit-exact, but its output goes through the DPU's requant and is **int8**. A reduction
expressed as a matmul by a ones vector — which is how the RK3588 builds RMSNorm, softmax's
row sums and every ReduceMean — needs an accumulator wider than its operands, and an int8
output saturates on the first long row. The 32-bit writer exists
(`rocket_matmul_int8_rk3576_i32()`) but it is the wrong instrument to put inside a
per-layer norm: it carries the wide-output poisoning, so it pays a power cycle per submit;
it drops atoms at a rate the memory system sets, so it needs a per-atom sentinel and an
arithmetic re-check; and its surface is bounded at 8 KiB per row task. That is a decoded,
gated research path, not a foundation for a hot inner op.

**So the call is: on this part the NPU runs convolution, int8 GEMM, pooling and LUT
activations, and the transformer composition stays on the CPU.** The three pieces that
argue for it are all on the convolution/DPU side rather than the matmul side — the direct
and depthwise int8 convolutions and the int8 first conv are bit-exact and 10-76x a scalar
CPU reference, the PPU gives pooling as an independent 31-write program in the cube the
conv path already packs, and the DPU LUT gives the nonlinear activations. That is a
CNN/detection/classification stack, and it is the one this silicon is shaped for.

All four pieces that decision named are now built and gated: per-output-channel weight
scales on the direct and depthwise paths and through a graph, the PPU pooling emitter, the
DPU LUT and its activation entry, and a whole network. Revisit the transformer question
only if a use for it appears — and if it does, the thing to settle first is whether an
int32 accumulation path can be made cheap enough to sit inside a norm, not whether the
fp16 one can be made faster.

## A whole network

MobileNetV1-224 at int8 runs end to end on this part — 27 convolutions, a PPU pooling
layer and a classifier — and returns TFLite's own label. Every one of the 29 compute
layers is **bit-exact against a CPU model of the part's own arithmetic**, and the whole
graph is **about 12 ms** per inference with resident weights and the tensors kept in cube
layout between layers (11.9-12.3 over three interleaved runs of 100) — 21 ms with the
transposes at every layer boundary, 115 ms without resident weights at all, and the
sections below are how the difference was found and what each part of it was.
`tests/rk3576_net_gate.c` is the gate; `tests/data/rk3576-net/mknet.py` flattens a
per-tensor-quantized `.tflite` into the blob it reads, carrying TFLite's output for
**every intermediate tensor** so a wrong layer is named by its own index rather than by a
wrong label 27 layers later.

**Against TFLite the graph differs on 0.095% of elements, every one of them by a single
count**, and the pooling layer and the classifier match exactly. That residue is the
requant and not a defect: this DPU's `OUT_CVT` carries a 15-bit multiplier and rounds
ties to even, where TFLite carries 31 bits and rounds half away from zero, so two
fixed-point approximations of one real-valued scale disagree on the elements near a
boundary. Fed its own previous output rather than TFLite's, a layer's disagreements
compound down the graph — by the classifier only 76% of the logits are bit-identical —
and the **top-1 and the whole top-5 still agree**. Which is the useful statement about a
quantized classifier: the ranking survives what the last bit does not.

**The fused RELU6 is free.** Every intermediate tensor is quantized at scale 6/255 with
its zero point at the bottom of the range, so the activation's clamp is exactly the int8
saturation the DPU already applies. The gate asserts that rather than assuming it.

**Three things a per-op gate structurally could not see**, all found by running the graph:

- **The two dead CBUF rungs** above. Every shape in the correctness envelope sat at F=0;
  the first plane large enough to need a rung was this network's second depthwise block.
- **TFLite's SAME padding is ASYMMETRIC, and it is an OUTPUT EXTENT rather than a
  materialised border.** A stride-2 SAME convolution over an even plane pads one row and
  one column at the END only. The CNA takes no configured trailing pad — it DERIVES the
  pad its last window consumes from the output extent and the leading pad — so that layer
  is a leading pad of zero against an extent one larger than the symmetric formula gives,
  and nothing has to be copied. Written up below; the first reading of this was that the
  border had to be materialised, and that buffer is what broke the cube chain.
- **The int8 first conv's bounds are jointly the ONNX symmetric-SAME convention**, and a
  TFLite SAME stem has `pad_left = 0`, so it does not satisfy them directly. The network
  runs its stem on the DIRECT path with the image widened to eight channels: the added
  channels hold the input zero point against zero weights, which contributes nothing to
  either half of the coefficient `A` fold and is exact for any zero point. That is not
  the last word — shifting the INPUT by one pixel against a pad of one moves the sample
  grid by a whole stride and reproduces TFLite's grid bit-exactly in the interior. That
  lowering is wired and gated but is a LOSS against a resident widened stem; it is
  written up above.

**Every layer of it runs on paths with no wide-output hazard.** The graph is int8
throughout, so it pays no poison guard and no power cycle, and the atom drop the int32
writers show did not appear — which is what makes this wall a wall and not a floor
with retries in it. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_net_gate.c`]

**THE GRAPH'S WALL IS HOST ARITHMETIC OVER THE WEIGHTS, NOT DISPATCH.** The whole
inference is **40 submits carrying 40 tasks**, and the three layers that cost most —
the stem and the two 1024-channel 1x1 convolutions — are 5, 1 and 1 of them. So output-
channel tiling, the obvious suspect when a layer is slow on a submit-floored part, is not
what those layers are spending: they were already one submit each. `ROCKET_RK3576_INT8_PROF=1`
splits a call into sums / feature pack / weight pack / coefficients / plan+gen / submit /
de-scatter, and across the graph the submit is **1-16% of a layer and 3% of the two most
expensive**, against a host share of 84-99%. Measure the submit COUNT before believing a
submit-floor story, and then measure the submit TIME before believing the count: the
~439 us floor is a floor on a round trip that waits for a full surface to drain, and
these jobs are well under it (0.13-0.25 ms each).

What the host is doing is two loops over the weight tensor, and **neither depends on the
input**: the per-output-channel filter sums fold the input zero point, and the cube scatter
is a pure re-layout. Straightened in place they took the graph from 146 ms to 115 ms.

**HOISTED OUT OF THE INFERENCE ENTIRELY, THEY AND THE BO CHURN THEY CARRIED ARE 65% OF THE
WALL: 98.8 ms -> 33.6 ms, a 2.94x speedup on the same 40 submits.** That is
`rocket_conv2d_int8_pack_rk3576()` — a handle a caller packs once and passes per inference
— and its answer is bit-identical to the per-call entry's: the graph's TFLite distance is
the same 4792 of 5044713 elements, every one of the 29 compute layers still bit-exact
against a CPU model of the part's own arithmetic, and the whole top-5 unchanged. It is one
code path, not a second implementation: a transient call builds the same handle and packs
each output-channel tile as its tile loop reaches it, so there is no second arithmetic to
keep in agreement. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_net_gate.c`,
`ROCKET_RK3576_NET_RESIDENT=1`]

**Only 29 ms of the 65 is the weight arithmetic the profile predicted.** Closing the
accounting is what found the rest — the profiler stopped at the last bucket and the
teardown happens after it, so 16 ms per inference had been attributed to nothing at all.
Per inference over 20 iterations:

| bucket | per-call | resident | removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| filter sums | 4.0 | 0.0 | 4.0 |
| feature pack | 27.8 | 11.6 | **16.2** |
| weight cube | 24.0 | 0.0 | 24.0 |
| coefficients | 0.6 | 0.0 | 0.6 |
| plan + gen | 6.7 | 2.2 | 4.5 |
| submit | 7.6 | 7.6 | 0.0 |
| de-scatter | 6.6 | 6.8 | 0.0 |
| **BO teardown** | **16.1** | 0.0 | **16.1** |
| library total | 93.3 | 28.3 | 65.0 |

Three things in that table are not what the earlier reading of this path expected.
**Unmapping the BOs a layer touched is 16 ms of a 99 ms graph** — a megabyte-sized dirty
mapping per tile, once per layer per inference — and it is invisible to any profile that
ends at the de-scatter. **The feature bucket more than halved without its scatter loop
changing at all**: 16 of its 28 ms were the allocation, the zeroing memset and the two
cache-maintenance ioctls, so the answer to "is the stem's feature pack the scatter?" is no,
most of it was the buffer. And **`plan+gen` was mostly an allocation too** — the regcmd BO.
The scatter that remains, 11.6 ms, is the transpose itself — vectorized below.

After it the graph is 33.6 ms and the submit is **22% of the library time rather than 8%**,
so this path has moved much closer to the dispatch story it was wrongly assumed to be in.
What is left on the host is the feature scatter (11.6), the de-scatter (6.8) and the
2.2 ms the table calls `plan + gen`.

**And that 2.2 ms is not the register program.** A resident handle's DMA addresses do not
move between calls, so the whole per-row-task stream is a pure function of frozen state
and can be generated once at pack time — built, gated bit-identical over the 173-shape
resident A/B and the whole network, and **worth 0.13 ms of 33.6**. What the bucket
actually holds is the OUTPUT SURFACE'S SENTINEL STAMP: the same bucket is 2.06 ms per
inference with the stamp on and **0.02 ms with it off**, so neither the row plan nor the
emitter is measurable at all. Cutting the stamp's `memset` from the whole surface to 64
bytes moves it 1.53 -> 1.45. The stamp itself is load-bearing: it is what makes "this task
never wrote" a property of the surface, and a conv inherits the wide-output poisoning from
whatever ran before it — so the lever is WHERE it is written, not whether.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, 2026-07-29]

**The next call's stamp now rides the de-scatter's own bracket** — the surface is already
inside a `PREP_BO`/`FINI_BO` pair there, so a resident tile pays one round trip per call
instead of two. Interleaved bench-100 runs of two binaries over five pairs: **30.2 ms
against 29.7**, and four sequential blocks of 100 each way put 29.9-30.6 against 29.8 and
below with no overlap. About **0.5 ms of 30**, never negative.

**And that corrects the attribution above.** "Two cache ioctls" predicts that removing one
of the two brackets removes half the cost, and it does not: with the sentinel off entirely
the same bench is **28.5 ms**, so the whole stamp costs 1.7 ms in the old arrangement and
1.2 in the new one. The fill's OWN cache maintenance follows it into whichever bracket it
lands in — `dma_sync_sgtable_for_device` walks the buffer whatever is dirty in it, which is
also why the 64-byte memset bought almost nothing. What the fold removes is one ioctl PAIR
per tile per call, and that is the 0.5 ms.

**The bucket profile is the wrong instrument for a lever this size.** Under
`ROCKET_RK3576_INT8_PROF=1` the two buckets are not additive with the wall: the
sentinel-off run reads WORSE in `plan+gen` plus `de-scatter` than either stamped
arrangement while being 1.2 ms FASTER in the wall, because the stamp also warms the output
surface for the de-scatter that follows it and that saving lands in a different bucket than
its cost. Long interleaved runs of the whole graph are what separated the two.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, 2026-07-29]

### The CHW <-> NC1HWC2 transpose is a 16x16 block, and eight live vectors is the point

The cube interleaves sixteen channels into every sixteen-byte atom, so a row-major tensor
and a cube are a **transpose** and not a copy. Written an element at a time that is one
useful byte per destination cache line, and the sixteen channels of a group each walk the
same lines again — four line touches per byte moved. A **16x16 block** does the same work
in whole vectors: sixteen 16-byte loads, four levels of `TRN1`/`TRN2`, four full cache
lines stored, every line touched once.

**Eight live vectors, not sixteen.** A 16x16 written as one array of sixteen `uint8x16_t`
needs thirty-two registers with its scratch, so the compiler puts the array on the STACK
and the whole point is lost — measured on the part at **3x slower** than the same
transpose done as two 8x8 halves plus a swap of the off-diagonal quadrants, which never
has more than eight live. Against the scalar loop, at `ic=128 px=3136`: pack 0.42 -> 0.23
as an array of sixteen and -> **0.078** as quadrants; unpack 0.35 -> 0.21 -> **0.095**.

The full-group predicate is a **constant argument to one `always_inline` helper**, so the
compiler emits the unpredicated sixteen-channel loop and the partial one from one source
rather than from two hand-written kernels. A per-lane runtime predicate costs 10-25%.
Dead lanes are still WRITTEN on the way in: they carry the cube's channel padding, which
must be zero, and a whole atom is stored either way.

**On the graph it is 1.41x**, bit-identical — the same 4792-element TFLite distance, the
same top-5, the same 173-shape resident A/B:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| feature scatter | 10.91 | 4.94 |
| de-scatter (readback) | 7.90 | 5.30 |
| submit | 7.17 | 7.22 |
| library total | 26.26 | 17.70 |
| **inference** | **29.5-30.3** | **20.3-21.3** |

**The submit is now the largest single bucket** at 41% of the library time, for the first
time on this path.

**What is left in those two buckets is the bytes, not the loop.** The de-scatter's
`PREP_BO` — the DPU write drain — is only **0.53 ms** of the 5.3, measured rather than
assumed, so the transpose really is the rest. At ~5 MB each way the two buckets run at
2.1-2.4 GB/s against this part's single-thread `memcpy` ceiling of **4.4 GB/s**
(`build/membench`, 256 MB): within about 2x of the cost of moving the bytes at all, where
before they were at 6-9x. Further vectorization is worth little.

### A tensor stays in CUBE LAYOUT between consecutive layers

The transposes above do not have to be paid at all between two NPU layers. A direct
conv's output surface stride is `ow*oh` exactly — `rocket_rk3576_out_surf_elems` pads only
the depthwise case — and a feature cube's channel-group stride is `ih*iw`, so **layer n's
output surface IS layer n+1's feature cube, byte for byte**, whenever the plane and the
channel rounding agree.

It is an API question and not a hardware one. Both ends are properties of the resident
handle, which already owns the input and output BOs and already freezes the fd:
`rocket_conv2d_int8_cube_of_rk3576()` describes a handle's output surface,
`_cube_in_rk3576()` makes another handle read from it instead of scattering, and
`_cube_out_rk3576()` leaves the output where the DPU wrote it. `in` and `out` may be NULL
for the end a handle no longer uses. A cube-in call does no cache maintenance either —
nothing on the CPU touches those lines, so the scatter's `PREP_BO`/`FINI_BO` pair goes
with it.

**Four bounds are refusals rather than fix-ups**, each naming a shape where the two
buffers are not the same object:

- **More than one output-channel tile.** Each tile owns its own surface BO and several BOs
  are not one cube. Nothing in a MobileNet reaches it: a 1x1 layer's weight slice is 32 KiB
  against the 144 KiB that leaves the tile at the whole layer.
- **A padded surface stride.** The depthwise path writes `round4(ow*oh)` per channel group,
  so its output is a direct conv's input cube only where the plane is already a multiple of
  four. 112, 56, 28 and 14 are; **7x7 is not**, and that is where the chain breaks.
- **An input channel count that is not a multiple of 32.** A handle's own cube relies on the
  zero it memset into the padding channels; a producer's surface holds whatever the DPU
  wrote there.
- A mismatched fd, plane or stride.

**The linking has to be done as a PRE-PASS over already-packed handles**, not decided as
the graph runs: the producer's "leave it in the cube" and the consumer's "read it from
there" are one decision, because a producer that skipped its de-scatter for a consumer
that then refused the cube would have left no row-major tensor at all.

On MobileNetV1-224 that is **24 joins of 30 layers** and takes the graph
**20.9-21.4 -> 11.9-12.3 ms, 1.73x**, on the same 40 submits and 40 tasks — with the
asymmetric-pad lowering below, which is what closes four of the joins. The two compose
superadditively; measured interleaved at `bench 100`:

| asymmetric pad | cube chain | ms per inference |
|---|---|---|
| materialised | off | 20.9-21.4 |
| extent | off | 20.1-20.5 |
| materialised | on | 15.7-16.4 |
| **extent** | **on** | **11.9-12.3** |

| bucket | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| submit | 8.4 | 7.5 |
| feature scatter | 6.5 | 3.0 |
| de-scatter | 5.5 | 0.8 |
| plan+gen (carries the stamp) | 0.8 | 2.6 |

**Gate it against the ARITHMETIC.** A layout change owes byte-identity: run the graph
row-major, snapshot every layer, link the cubes, run it again, and compare every layer the
second run still materialises plus the final logits. A wrong byte inside a join reaches the
next materialisation point, so nothing hides in the middle of a chain. Measured: every
materialised layer identical, the logits byte-identical, the top-5 unchanged, the
4792-element TFLite distance unmoved.

**The SUBMIT is now 62% of the library time** — 40 submits of about 190 us each, well under
the ~440 us floor, so the lever there is the submit COUNT across layers and not the floor.
**A submit removed is worth about 110 us on this graph**, measured rather than inferred:
`ROCKET_RK3576_BATCH_TASKS=1` chains a tile's row tasks into one hardware kick, which takes
the graph from 40 submits to 29 and from 11.9-12.2 ms to 10.4-11.3 — the same 40 tasks, the
same assertions. That is 8-12% here against about 3% over the per-op conv gate, because a
graph's layers are now cheap enough for the submit to dominate. Chaining ACROSS layers is
the same shape of lever and about 3 ms more, but it is a graph-level object rather than a
per-handle one: the chain rewrite lays programs out inside ONE BO, and a layer's regcmd
buffer, its output extents and its poison guard are all per handle today.
The sentinel stamp is 0.9-1.9 ms of the 12 whichever path it rides: on the row-major path it
is the fill inside the de-scatter's bracket, and on the cube path its own `PREP_BO`/`FINI_BO`
pair at the start of the next call, because the surface is read as an input before that
handle runs again. The two cost the same, so the cube chain does not make the guard more
expensive. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, 2026-07-29]

### An asymmetric pad is an OUTPUT EXTENT, not a materialised border

**The CNA has no configured trailing pad.** It takes the pad its LAST WINDOW CONSUMES,
derived from the output extent and the leading pad — `r76_trail_pad()` in the emitter is
that derivation, and `CNA_PAD_CON0`'s bottom and right fields carry the result. So TFLite's
SAME at an even input and stride two, `pad_before = 0` and `pad_after = 1`, is a leading pad
of **zero** against an output extent one larger than the symmetric formula gives. Nothing
has to be copied.

That geometry was already inside the int8 correctness envelope: six shapes of
`tests/rk3576_conv_shapes.h` carry a zero leading pad against a consumed trailing one,
direct and depthwise, and all are bit-exact. What kept it out of a graph was the
DESCRIPTOR, which could only say "derive the output from a symmetric pad" — so a graph
runner materialised the border into a row-major buffer, and that buffer is what broke the
cube chain at every stride-2 depthwise layer.

`rocket_conv2d_desc.oh/.ow` now name the extent, zero meaning "whatever
`rocket_conv2d_oh/ow` derive" — every caller that memsets the struct, so nothing existing
changes — and `rocket_conv2d_trail_y/_x` are the pad it implies. **The bound is the
KERNEL**: a trailing pad of `kh` or more is an output row whose whole window is pad, and it
is refused. The fp16 RK3576 path, both packed-image paths and `rocket_conv2d_plan` (the
RK3588 fp16 generator) refuse an extent they did not derive themselves, because that
geometry is claimed at int8 and measured nowhere else.

On MobileNetV1-224 five layers pad asymmetrically — the stem and the four stride-2
depthwise — the derived trailing pad matches the model on 30 of 30 compute layers, and every
layer stays bit-exact against a CPU model of the part's own arithmetic.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, 2026-07-29]

**TRAP: a per-layer reference has to be told the same lowering.** The gate's per-axis pass
builds its own descriptor; left without the extent, its entry computed 55 rows while its
reference computed 56, and the five asymmetric layers reported maxdiff 255 at 5-29% exact —
a lowering disagreement that reads exactly like a hardware fault.

**A depthwise weight zero point rides in the WEIGHT CUBE**, which is what let the graph's
thirteen depthwise layers run at all: every per-tensor TFLite depthwise filter carries
one, and the 48-byte depthwise coefficient group has no `B` field to fold it into. It does
not need one. The int8 depthwise cube gives every (channel, tap) **two live bytes and the
datapath adds them**, so the effective weight is a nine-bit value and `w - w_zp` is
carried exactly across the pair, bounded only by the pair's own [-256, 254]. The
coefficient `A` already computes `-in_zp*(sum_w - w_zp*taps)`, which is the fold a
pre-centred cube wants, so nothing downstream changes. Re-centring in int8 instead is NOT
available — 12 of this model's 13 depthwise layers overflow — and re-quantizing to a
symmetric scale would have been an accuracy loss of up to 1.65x coarsening rather than an
exact answer. Bit-exact at both signs, at both channel-group boundaries, at k=5 and at
stride 2. [HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_conv_lib_gate.c` group `zp`]

**Per-axis weight quantization reaches the depthwise path, and depthwise is the cheaper
half of it.** The 48-byte depthwise coefficient group carries the same per-output-channel
int16 `C` the 64-byte one does; the planner was wired to the direct path's group and
nothing else stood in the way. What makes depthwise the easy case is the accumulator
bound: `128*sum|w|` over `kh*kw` taps rather than `ic*kh*kw`, one to two orders of
magnitude smaller, so the `C` ramp reaches the int16 field's own 32767 ceiling and the
gain resolution a single `OUT_CVT` shift leaves is finer than the direct path's at the
same channel count. It is also ONE task whatever the channel count — output channel `c`
is bound to input channel `c`, so there is no output-channel window to program and no
scale sort to make, and the accuracy comes without the submits the direct path spends on
it. Measured over eight shapes (kernels 1/3/5, both strides, 32-256 channels): the worst
distance to an exact per-axis requant is **0.5 counts at spreads to 100x and 0.8 at
1000x**, against 0.5-1.9 on the direct path. A MobileNet is mostly depthwise, which is
what a per-axis model needs from this path.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_perchannel_gate.c`]

**AND IT RUNS AS A GRAPH, WHERE THE ERROR DOES NOT COMPOUND.** A per-axis requant's error
is a per-layer quantity, so what one layer gives up to the single `OUT_CVT` shift is not
what twenty-nine of them give up, and only a network answers that. `rk3576_net_gate
peraxis` runs MobileNetV1-224 with every convolution and depthwise convolution on the
per-axis entry, chained.

The per-axis model comes from re-quantizing this model's OWN weights per output channel —
what a TFLite int8 converter does to the same float tensor: each channel moves from the
shared grid onto `max|w_c - w_zp| * w_scale / 127`, with its weights and bias rescaled and
the activations keeping the model's per-tensor scales and zero points. The spread that
produces is real and wide: **150x at the stem, 145x and 129x at the early depthwise
layers, 105x at layer 25**.

Every one of the 29 compute layers is within **ONE COUNT** of an exact per-axis requant —
87.7% to 99.9% of elements identical to it — and the chained network returns TFLite's own
top-1 AND its whole top-5. The per-op gate's worst-case reading of 26.6 counts stands
where it was measured (a synthetic 100x spread at ic=128 with the whole layer in one
tile); a real model's spread does not land there, because the planner sizes `C` against
the ACTUAL accumulator bound and this model's wide-scale channels are also its
small-accumulator ones. The output-channel tile lever is **never taken**: the predicted
worst-case gain error is 0.003%-0.82% per layer, under the 1% target, so the planner keeps
each layer in one task and pays no extra submit.

What that pass asserts is what a graph is for — no layer refuses, no layer is far enough
from an exact per-axis requant to be a defect rather than the single shift, and the
compounded chain still returns the framework's label. The bit-exact assertion against the
chip's own arithmetic stays in `rk3576_perchannel_gate`, where the planner's `C`
multipliers and `OUT_CVT` shift are visible to a model; they are not visible to a graph.
[HW sweep, H96 MAX M9, `tests/rk3576_net_gate.c peraxis`]
